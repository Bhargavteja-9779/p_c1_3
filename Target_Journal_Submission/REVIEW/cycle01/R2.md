# R2: Battery domain expert review (electrochemistry, cell-testing practice)

Manuscript: "Stopping Cycle-Life Tests Early With Guaranteed Error Rates ..." (manuscript/Draft_Manuscript.md, SI, code/src/batscreen).

I checked the claims below against the code, the feature files (data/features/*.npz) and the split-level results (results/raw/E1_*_cells.csv.gz, E5). The raw .mat and .pkl files are not in the container, so anything that depends on them is marked UNVERIFIED. The scripts I used for the checks are short pandas/numpy snippets over those files, and the commands are summarised under each finding.

Summary of counts: CRITICAL 0, MAJOR 5, MINOR 12, NIT 3.

## MAJOR

### R2-01 (MAJOR): The use case does not match how cells are actually qualified, and the paper does not say what a "passed" cell is for
**Location:** Sec. 1 (para 2), Sec. 3.1, Sec. 3.5 (choice of L*), Sec. 5 "Recommended workflow", Abstract.

**Evidence.**
- **How qualification is done in practice.** Industrial cycle-life qualification (OEM DV/PV, IEC 62660-1, the USABC/DOE test manuals) is a decision about a *design or lot*. It uses a small sample (typically 3 to 10 cells per condition), periodic reference performance tests and reliability statistics such as a success run or a Weibull lower bound. Individual production cells are never cycled for hundreds of cycles to be "passed", because cycling consumes the life being certified, and cycled cells are not shipped. Per-cell production screening uses formation, OCV/self-discharge (k-value) and ACIR instead.
- **What the data sets are.** In MATR/CLO and HUST the meaningful unit is the *charging or discharging protocol*. HUST has one cell per protocol (77/77). MATR/CLO has 78 unique policy strings for 169 cells, and 43 policies have a single cell (my check on `policy` in matr_clo_severson.npz). So the per-cell guarantee is in practice a per-protocol guarantee for an R&D campaign like Attia's CLO, not a guarantee for cell qualification.
- **The specification L\* is a statistical choice, not an engineering one.** L\* is set "close to the median lifetimes, so that both classes are well represented" (Sec. 3.5, line 100). That makes the screened population about 50% defective. No qualification population looks like that. At realistic yields, passing needs at least 9 bad calibration cells (Remark iii). With a bad-cell prevalence π, that means about 9/π calibration cells: 180 cells at 5% prevalence, 900 at 1%, all cycled to L\*.
- **The error levels are lax for qualification.** α = β = 0.10 means accepting a 10% false-pass rate. At α = 0.05 the rule needs n0 ≥ 19 bad calibration cells; at α = 0.01 it needs n0 ≥ 99.
- **Calibration duplicates the qualification.** The calibration campaign has to cycle at least 10 good cells of the *same population* to L\* (workflow step 3). That is already a population-level demonstration that the design meets L\*. The paper should explain what extra decision the per-cell screen then supports.

**Fix.**
- Reframe the primary use case as R&D campaigns that screen test conditions or design variants, where each unit is a protocol or variant drawn from a design space (as in Attia et al. and E9), and say explicitly that this is not production-cell qualification.
- Add a paragraph and a table relating the calibration-set size to the bad-cell prevalence π and to α (n0 ≥ ⌈1/α⌉ − 1, so roughly 9/π cells in total).
- Discuss how the method relates to reliability demonstration testing (success-run or Weibull plans) and to the standards above.
- Justify L\* from an application, or present L\* = median explicitly as a design choice for the benchmark.

### R2-02 (MAJOR): The cost model counts channel-cycles, but the paper motivates the method with calendar time. The campaign's wall-clock duration is usually unchanged, and calibration has to finish before screening can start
**Location:** Abstract ("78.3% ... saved"); Sec. 1, line 19 ("slowest step ... weeks to months"); Sec. 4.3; Sec. 6 "Cost model"; Conclusions.

**Evidence.**
- **The campaign still lasts L\* cycles in most splits.** Any undecided good cell keeps the campaign running until cycle L\*. Under the proposed rule, the last test cell finished at L\* in **73.1% of MATR/CLO splits and 90.1% of HUST splits**, and the median campaign end was 750 and 1,900 cycles, i.e. exactly L\*. My command was: groupby split, take the maximum `time`, method == proposed, on E1_*_cells.csv.gz. On average 13.3% (MATR/CLO) and 39.5% (HUST) of test cells per split were never decided by the model. The "78.3% saved" is therefore channel-time freed, not time-to-result.
- **Calibration and screening are sequential.** The thresholds exist only after the calibration campaign (L\* cycles) has finished. The cost of that campaign, and the fact that it must precede or run in parallel with screening, does not appear in the savings figure.
- **Cycles are not a unit of time.** MATR protocols with higher charge rates have shorter cycles, and these are also the short-lived cells. In HUST, 77 different multi-step discharge protocols give cycles of different durations. RPTs, rests and channel down-time are ignored (the authors acknowledge this in Sec. 6, but the headline numbers still use cycles).

**Fix.**
- Report three quantities separately:
  - (a) channel-hours saved, converting cycles to hours from the recorded time or charge-time data (`chargetime` is already loaded, and the discharge duration follows from the rate);
  - (b) campaign makespan: when the last cell's decision is known;
  - (c) total programme time including calibration.
- Optionally simulate a cycler with N channels and a queue of cells, to show throughput gains when freed channels are refilled.
- Change the Abstract and Conclusions so that "saved" refers to channel time.

### R2-03 (MAJOR): MATR/CLO class labels are strongly confounded with test batch, and the pooled error rates hide per-batch error rates above the nominal level
**Location:** Sec. 3.5, Table 1, Sec. 4.2 and 4.3, Sec. 5 (iii) ("plausible for cells drawn from the same production lot").

**Evidence (computed from data/features/matr_clo_severson.npz and E1_matr_clo_cells.csv.gz, method = proposed).**
- **Share of bad cells (life < 750) by batch:** b1 41%, **b2 100%** (43/43 bad, median life 481), **b3 7.5%** (3/40 bad), b4/CLO 40%. Batch b3 is also a different protocol structure: all 40 of its policy strings contain "newstructure".
- **The b2 cells are what make the pooled result look good.** They were decided by the model 99.3% of the time, 82% by cycle 40, with 91% of cycling saved and a false-pass frequency of 0.024. These cells are 43 of the 81 bad cells.
- **Per-batch class-conditional error frequencies** (each cell's error rate averaged over the splits in which it was a test cell; cell-bootstrap 95% CI):

| Batch | Class | Cells | Error frequency | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| b1 | good | 24 | FRR 0.157 | 0.069–0.262 |
| b4 (CLO) | bad | 18 | FPR 0.132 | 0.035–0.255 |
| b3 | bad | 3 | FPR 0.123 | 0.047–0.244 |
| b2 | bad | 43 | FPR 0.024 | 0.003–0.061 |

  The single-checkpoint rule had a false-pass frequency of 0.266 (0.125–0.431) on CLO bad cells.
- **Within-batch feature strength is uneven.** The Spearman correlation of dq_var with log life at cycle 40 is −0.86 in b1 but only −0.44 in b2 and −0.22 in b4.
- **Why this matters.** A laboratory screens one batch or lot at a time. The marginal guarantee over a mixture of batches says nothing about the error rate for a given batch, and the efficiency shown at 20 to 40 cycles comes largely from recognising the all-bad batch b2.

**Fix.**
- Report FPR, FRR and savings per batch.
- Add an analysis restricted to batches with both classes present (b1 and b4), or calibrate with batch as a Mondrian stratum.
- Discuss the batch and lifetime confound explicitly.
- Weaken "plausible for cells drawn from the same production lot": the MATR batches are test campaigns with different protocol structures, not production lots.

### R2-04 (MAJOR): The end-of-life threshold is described as "the same convention" in both data sets, but it means different things physically and is rate-dependent
**Location:** Sec. 3.5 ("Both datasets record data until the discharge capacity reaches 0.88 Ah (80% of nominal)"), Sec. 6 "Lifetime labels", hust.py docstring ("the same end-of-life convention as the MATR data").

**Evidence.**
- **Initial capacities differ.** The median capacity at cycle 2 (`q2`) is 1.18 Ah for HUST (10th–90th percentile 1.17–1.20; range 1.157–1.231), but 1.05–1.08 Ah for the MATR batches.
- **So the 0.88 Ah threshold corresponds to different fade levels.** EOL is at about **75% of initial capacity for HUST** and about **82–84% for MATR/CLO**.
- **HUST capacity is measured at different discharge rates.** Each HUST cell's capacity comes from its own multi-step discharge protocol, so both the initial capacity and the EOL crossing depend on the rate (rate-capacity effect).
- **Consequence for the HUST predictor.** In HUST, q2 alone has Spearman ρ = +0.53 with log life at every checkpoint; in MATR/CLO ρ = −0.10. Cells that show more capacity at their own discharge rate have more headroom above 0.88 Ah. Part of the HUST "lifetime" is therefore a measurement-rate artefact of the nominal-based threshold, mixed with the real benefit of milder discharge.
- **The MATR measurement is also unconventional.** The MATR "capacity" is the 4C operating-discharge capacity, not an RPT capacity at C/3 or 1C, so EOL mixes impedance rise with capacity loss.

**Fix.**
- State the relative EOL for each data set.
- Note that capacity is measured at the operating discharge rate and is rate-dependent.
- Add a sensitivity run with EOL defined relative to each cell's initial capacity (e.g. 80% of q2), which needs only the stored capacity trajectories.
- Remove "the same end-of-life convention".

### R2-05 (MAJOR): The most relevant domain literature and baselines are missing, so the gap statement is overstated
**Location:** Sec. 2 ("These works report prediction error at a fixed checkpoint; none provides a decision rule with a pre-specified, guaranteed error rate"), Sec. 3.4, reference list.

**Evidence and missing items.**
- **Severson et al.'s own classifier.** Severson et al. [1] also built a *classification* model that splits cells at 550 cycles using only the first 5 cycles (about 4.9% test error). This is exactly an early pass/reject screen against L\*. The authors' own literature matrix (literature/literature_matrix.md:14) records it ("also binary classification at cycle 5"), but the manuscript never mentions it, and the only Severson-style baseline is point regression at cycle 100.
- **Attia et al.'s early stopping.** The CLO work [2] stopped tests early on the basis of predictions. How its decision errors were handled deserves discussion.
- **Feature-engineering work on the same data.** Fei et al. (Energy 2021) and Paulson et al. (J. Power Sources 2022) are absent from the references and from references_verified.json (grep returned 0 hits).
- **Cell-to-cell variation and test sample size.** This literature bears directly on calibration-set sizes and exchangeability: Baumhöfer et al. (J. Power Sources 2014, production-caused variation and its correlation with initial performance), Harris, Harris & Li (J. Power Sources 2017, Weibull failure statistics for 24 cells), and Dechent et al. (Batteries & Supercaps 2021, degradation-test sample sizes). None is cited.
- **No testing standards.** No qualification standard (IEC 62660-1, IEC 61960, USABC manual) is cited, although the paper is framed around "qualification".

**Fix.**
- Add Severson's cycle-5 classifier at L\* = 550 as a baseline. E7 already varies L\*, so it can be run at 550 on MATR.
- Cite and discuss the works above.
- Rephrase the gap as "no decision rule with a finite-sample, class-conditional guarantee under sequential stopping".

## MINOR

### R2-06 (MINOR): Removing the five batch-1 cells that never reached EOL contradicts the paper's own thesis that truncated cells suffice
**Location:** Sec. 3.5; SI line 24 ("final discharge capacity 0.91–0.97 Ah, i.e. end of life never reached"); matr.py:27.

**Evidence.** Under the paper's framework, a cell censored after L\* = 750 cycles is a known *good* cell and is fully usable for calibration and testing. Remark (i) makes exactly this point. Dropping these cells removes long-lived cells, a form of selection bias, and contradicts the "truncated campaigns" message.

**Fix.** Report each removed cell's number of recorded cycles (UNVERIFIED here, because the raw files are absent). Keep every cell with more than L\* recorded cycles as a good cell. The same reasoning undercuts the Limitations reason for excluding the Sandia data ("many cells not reaching end of life"); see R2-12.

### R2-07 (MINOR): The statement about data recording is inaccurate
**Location:** Sec. 3.5, line 100 ("Both datasets record data until the discharge capacity reaches 0.88 Ah").

**Evidence.** The code contradicts this for MATR. matr.py:95 says "some batch-2 cells were cycled beyond end of life, so records may be longer". Five b1 records are also merged with b2 continuations.

**Fix.** State that MATR records may extend beyond EOL and that labels are the providers' `cycle_life` values. For the merged cells, give the junction rule (`life + l2 - 1`, matr.py:65). Show that it reproduces the published lifetimes: my check gives b1c0–c4 = 1852, 2160, 2237, 1434 and 1709. Note that the longest calibration cells, and hence the factor 2.61 (R2-17), are exactly these merged cells.

### R2-08 (MINOR): The protocol counts are inconsistent
**Location:** Sec. 3.5 ("72 fast-charging policies"), Table 1 ("69"), Sec. 4.6/E9 ("78 MATR/CLO charging protocols").

**Evidence.** The npz file has 69 unique policy strings among the 124 MATR cells and 78 including CLO.

**Fix.** Explain the gap from Severson's 72 (string conventions, or the removed cells) and use one number throughout.

### R2-09 (MINOR): "Every rule failed on later batches" is not true, and the "batch shift" is really a protocol-structure shift
**Location:** Sec. 4.6, line 206.

**Evidence.** In SI Table S7 (line 334), the cycle-100 point rule on b3 with source calibration had FPR 0.055 (upper 0.072) and FRR 0.075 (upper 0.087), i.e. within the targets. Batch b3 has only 3 bad cells, so its FPR is very noisy. Batch b3 also uses the "newstructure" protocol family, which changes rests and protocol structure.

**Fix.** Write "every rule failed on at least one target batch". Describe the shift as a change of test campaign and protocol structure, not of manufacturing lot. Note that ΔQ(V) features at 4C are sensitive to that protocol structure, which is itself a practical warning.

### R2-10 (MINOR): The physical explanation for the Q(V) glitches is not substantiated, and the fix was chosen after looking at outcome correlations
**Location:** Sec. 3.2 ("some cycles are non-standard (rest or diagnostic steps)"); EXPERIMENT_LOG line 9 ("Q(V) features at checkpoints 200/250 decorrelated from lifetime ... Fix: element-wise median").

**Evidence.** Neither the manuscript nor the log identifies which cycles are affected, in which cells, or what steps were involved. The 5-cycle median was adopted because features had lost correlation with lifetime, which is an outcome-informed preprocessing choice.

**Fix.**
- List the affected cycles and cells, with an SI figure showing raw and median Q(V).
- Cite the provider documentation if rests or diagnostic steps really exist.
- Report E1 results with window = 1 (the code supports it via `matr_features(window=1)`) as a sensitivity check.

### R2-11 (MINOR, UNVERIFIED): The HUST V(q) construction may not mean what the text says
**Location:** hust.py:119–131; Sec. 3.2 ("grid of discharged capacity q").

**Evidence.** The code uses `q = cap.max() - cap` and calls it "discharged capacity, monotone non-decreasing".
- If the "Capacity (mAh)" column counts *up* during discharge, as on Neware-style cyclers, q is the charge remaining until end of discharge, not the charge discharged.
- If the column resets at each step of the multi-step discharge, the curve is corrupted.
- In addition, if step transitions are triggered by voltage or capacity, they shift in q as the cell fades, which puts step-change IR artefacts into dV(q).

The mean dV at cycle 1000 grows to −35 mV at the high-q end, which fits either reading.

**Fix.** State and verify the convention against the raw columns and the step indices. Plot V(q) for one cell at cycles 10 and 1000 with the step boundaries marked.

### R2-12 (MINOR): The generality claims go beyond the evidence
**Location:** Title ("Lithium-Ion Cells"), Abstract ("246 commercial LiFePO4/graphite cells from two laboratories"), Sec. 6 "One chemistry".

**Evidence.**
- Both data sets use the *same cell model* (A123 APR18650M1A, Table 1 caption). "Two laboratories" therefore adds protocol and test-rig diversity, not cell diversity.
- The ΔQ(V) features rely on LFP's flat plateau and on a constant 4C discharge.
- NMC/NCA cells often show knees (cf. [6]) that are poorly predicted from early cycles, so the efficiency, and possibly the practicality, of the method may collapse there.
- The Sandia data were excluded because "many cells [were] not reaching end of life". The method's selling point is that such cells are usable when they pass L\*.

**Fix.** Say "a single commercial LFP/graphite cell model" in the Abstract and Conclusions. Temper the language on generality. Either reconsider the Sandia exclusion, or give a reason that is consistent with the method.

### R2-13 (MINOR): Feature availability under realistic test plans is not discussed
**Location:** Sec. 3.2, Sec. 3.5 (checkpoints 20, 30, ...).

**Evidence.** The features come from high-resolution, every-cycle 4C operating discharges. Standard qualification plans measure capacity and impedance at periodic RPTs (every 50 to 100 cycles or every few weeks), and the operating profile is often a drive-cycle or partial-DoD profile from which Q(V) cannot be extracted. Checkpoints at 20 to 40 cycles, where half of the MATR/CLO decisions are made, would not exist under such a plan.

**Fix.** Add a discussion, and ideally an ablation with an RPT-like grid (e.g. 50, 100, 200, 300). E3 already has 50/100/200/300; tie it to this argument.

### R2-14 (MINOR): Some features describe the test protocol rather than the cell
**Location:** Sec. 3.2 (`chargetime5`, `tmax_mean`); features.py:235–238.

**Evidence.** The mean charge time of cycles 2–6 is set by the charging policy, and the maximum temperature largely follows the C-rate. These are legitimate covariates for protocol screening, but they carry no information when all screened cells share one protocol, which is the lot-screening case the paper invokes. Their univariate correlation with life is weak (Spearman ρ = −0.17 and −0.07 at cycle 20), so the effect is probably small (UNVERIFIED in the multivariate model).

**Fix.** Label these as protocol covariates and report an ablation without them.

### R2-15 (MINOR): Claims about practice are unsupported
**Location:** Sec. 1, line 19 ("Cycle-life testing is the slowest step"); Sec. 7 ("routinely used to make go/no-go decisions", "the common practice of re-checking predictions as cycling proceeds").

**Evidence.** No citation supports these. Calendar-ageing tests are usually slower than cycle-life tests.

**Fix.** Cite sources or soften the wording ("one of the slowest", "can be used").

### R2-16 (MINOR, partly UNVERIFIED): The comparison with Severson's 9.1% is not like-for-like
**Location:** Sec. 4.1, line 122.

**Evidence.** As far as I recall, Severson's reported test error excludes one outlier cell (the short-lived 148-cycle cell, b2c1 here, which this paper's Sec. 4.8 also singles out) and was measured on fixed primary and secondary test sets. The authors' 11.2% includes that cell and pools all data.

**Fix.** Check against the Severson Table 1 footnote and state the difference, or recompute the MAPE without b2c1.

### R2-17 (MINOR): The Abstract highlights the most favourable calibration-campaign metric
**Location:** Abstract ("calibration campaigns ended 2.61 times sooner"); Sec. 4.3 ("Total calibration cell-cycles fell by factors of 1.22 and 1.09").

**Evidence.** The factor 2.61 is a wall-clock figure set by the single longest calibration cell. Those cells are the merged b1/b2 cells at 1852–2237 cycles, whose lifetimes depend on the merge rule (R2-07). The cell-cycle saving is only 1.22 (MATR/CLO) and 1.09 (HUST).

**Fix.** Give both numbers in the Abstract.

## Overall recommendation: **major revision**

The statistical core is sound and the battery data are handled carefully: the cleaning steps match the original authors' scripts, and the merged lifetimes reproduce the known values. However, the paper's practical framing does not match battery-testing practice. Its efficiency and validity headlines rest on a batch-confounded pooled data set and on a cell-cycle cost metric that does not reflect time-to-result.

### The three changes that matter most
1. **Reframe the use case and the cost model (R2-01, R2-02).** Present the method as screening of R&D protocols or design variants. Report channel-hours, campaign makespan (currently equal to L\* in 73% and 90% of splits) and programme time including calibration. Relate the calibration-set size to the bad-cell prevalence and to α.
2. **Expose the MATR batch confound (R2-03).** Report error rates and savings per batch (b1 FRR 0.157; b4 FPR 0.132; b2 all-bad and trivially screened). Add within-batch or batch-stratified calibration results.
3. **Fix the EOL description and the literature (R2-04, R2-05).** EOL is about 75% of initial capacity for HUST versus about 82–84% for MATR, and it is rate-dependent; add a relative-EOL sensitivity run. Add Severson's cycle-5 classifier at L\* = 550 as a baseline, and cite Fei, Paulson, Baumhöfer, Harris, Dechent and the relevant testing standards.
