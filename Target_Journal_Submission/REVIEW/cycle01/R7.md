## Findings

**R7-01 (MAJOR). Holm correction is used but not cited, and the bibliography pipeline drops it.**
- Location: Draft_Manuscript.md:113 ("Holm correction over comparators"); Table 3 column "p (Holm)"; :182; SI Table S2.
- Evidence: there is no Holm entry in the rendered list. `holm1979` exists in references_verified.json with `verified: False` and no metadata, and it is never cited. The matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation and the normal-approximation CIs are also uncited.
- Fix: cite S. Holm, *Scand. J. Stat.* 1979, 6, 65-70 at line 113. Optionally cite Kerby (2014, *Compr. Psychol.* 3) for the rank-biserial correlation.

**R7-02 (MAJOR). The literatures that are closest to the method's novelty claim are missing.**
- Location: Related Work, "Statistical foundations" and "Gap" (Draft:38-40).
- Problem: the contribution is a threshold calibrated on the maximum over checkpoints of normalised scores, so that error holds under data-dependent stopping. Two literatures do essentially this, and a statistics-literate reviewer will ask for both:
  - (a) **Group-sequential designs**, which spend error across interim looks. A reviewer would expect Pocock 1977 (*Biometrika* 64:191); O'Brien & Fleming 1979 (*Biometrics* 35:549); Lan & DeMets 1983 (*Biometrika* 70:659); and the Jennison & Turnbull 2000 monograph. The naive-sequential vs Bonferroni vs max-statistic comparison is exactly the classic interim-analysis comparison. Citing only Wald's SPRT and confidence sequences understates the precedent.
  - (b) **Simultaneous / sup-type conformal scores over an index set.** These include conformal bands for functional data with a max-over-t nonconformity score: Lei, Rinaldo & Wasserman 2015 (*Ann. Math. Artif. Intell.* 74:29); Diquigiovanni, Fontana & Vantini 2022 (*J. Multivar. Anal.* 189:104879). They also include multi-horizon conformal forecasting: Stankeviciute, Alaa & van der Schaar, NeurIPS 2021, which uses Bonferroni across horizons and is the direct analogue of the paper's Bonferroni baseline.
- Also expected:
  - Learn-then-Test (Angelopoulos, Bates, Candès, Jordan, Lei, *Ann. Appl. Stat.* 2025) for calibrating decision thresholds with finite-sample error control.
  - Conformal p-values / outlier testing (Bates et al., *Ann. Stat.* 2023, 51:149).
  - Safe anytime-valid inference (Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk, Shafer, *Stat. Sci.* 2023).
- Fix: add these references. Rephrase the novelty statement (Draft:40) as an application or adaptation of max-statistic (simultaneous) calibration to truncated, class-conditional battery screening, not as a new mechanism.

**R7-03 (MAJOR). The intro claim about truncated calibration contradicts the paper's own Related Work, and the novelty overlaps with conformal survival analysis.**
- Location: Draft:21 and contribution 2 (Draft:26).
- Evidence: the intro says the finite-sample methods "are calibrated with cells cycled to EOL". Yet Draft:38 states that conformalised survival analysis [34-36] "uses lifetimes truncated at a cut-off to obtain valid lower bounds". Candès et al. [34] calibrate on T∧c0, which is the same device as using min(T, L\*) with c0 = L\*. So "each calibration cell needs to be cycled only to min(T, L\*)" is not new in itself.
- Fix: qualify the intro sentence to "conformal *lifetime intervals as used in battery work* [29,30, ...]". In contribution 2, state that truncated calibration follows the T∧c0 device of [34]. Here it is simpler because truncation is administrative (deterministic at L\*) rather than random censoring, so no censoring-weight estimation is needed. That precise distinction is the defensible contribution.

**R7-05 (MINOR). The Severson 9.1% figure is misattributed in the intro and over-stretched in Results.**
- Location: Draft:19 ("features of the discharge-capacity curve difference between cycles 10 and 100 predict ... with about 9% error") and Draft:122 ("11.2% ... is close to the 9.1% reported by Severson et al. for their full model on a fixed split").
- Evidence: 9.1% is the headline of the *full* model. That model adds discharge-capacity, internal-resistance, temperature and charge-time features to ΔQ. It is the error on the test sets *with one anomalous primary-test cell excluded*: full model 7.5% primary (14.1% with the outlier), 10.7% secondary. The ΔQ-variance-only model performs worse.
- Fix:
  - Draft:19: say "features derived from the first 100 cycles, chiefly the variance of ΔQ_{100-10}(V), ... 9.1% test error".
  - Draft:122: say "11.2% vs 9.1% (full model including resistance features, outlier cell excluded)". Call it "comparable" rather than "close". 11.2% is 23% higher in relative terms.

## Overall recommendation (citation lens): minor revision

The reference list is real and almost entirely correct in substance. No fabricated reference was detected, though two are unverified. The problems are fixable: the positioning against group-sequential and simultaneous-conformal methods, a missing statistical citation, and many outdated or erroneous bibliographic entries.

Three changes that matter most:
1. **R7-02/R7-03:** cite group-sequential designs, sup-type (functional / multi-horizon) conformal methods and Learn-then-Test. State plainly that truncated calibration is the T∧c0 device of Candès et al. Narrow the novelty claim accordingly.
2. **R7-04/R7-06/R7-01:** fix the bibliography. Use published venues for the 7 arXiv-cited papers, correct the years and pages, restore the Vovk authors, and add Holm 1979.
3. **R7-07/R7-05:** verify or replace the unverified [14] and [31]. Correct the Severson 9.1% attribution: it is the full model, outlier excluded, not ΔQ features alone.
