# R9: Writing, structure and presentation (cycle 01)

**Lens.** Can a *Battery Energy* reader (a battery engineer, not a statistician) follow, trust and use this paper? I read all of `manuscript/Draft_Manuscript.md` and `manuscript/Supporting_Information.md`, rendered all 33 PDF pages (`pdftoppm -r 60`, then montaged and viewed), and looked at every PNG in `figures/` at full resolution. I recomputed one consistency check from `results/raw/E1_*_splits.csv`. Line numbers refer to `Draft_Manuscript.md` (MS) or `Supporting_Information.md` (SI) unless marked "PDF l.".

**Overall impression.** The science is sound and the writing is precise. The paper still reads like a statistics paper with a battery dataset attached. It has five problems:
- The method is presented only in symbols, and the workflow schematic that exists in `figures/` is not used.
- The two main tables break badly in the PDF.
- The SI is mostly raw code-output dumps.
- Results are repeated three or four times across the abstract, Results, Discussion and Conclusions.
- Figure labels use internal code names (`all16`, `source_cal`, `s_k`).

None of this is fatal, but a journal reviewer from the battery community would ask for all of it to be fixed.

Severity counts: CRITICAL 0, MAJOR 9, MINOR 20, NIT 9.

**R9-01 (MAJOR): A battery engineer cannot follow the method without reading the proof. No intuitive picture is given, and the existing workflow figure is unused.**

- *Evidence:* The decision rule is stated only as order statistics: "$\hat\lambda=P_{(r_0)}$ … $r_0$ is the smallest integer not less than $(n_0+1)(1-\alpha)$". Nowhere does the paper say in words what the pass threshold *is*. The most intuitive figure, the score trajectories with pass and reject thresholds (`Fig6_score_trajectories.png`), was moved to the SI as Figure S1.
- *Fix:*
  1. Make `Fig1_workflow.png` (after fixing R9-19) the new Figure 1, cited at the start of §3.
  2. Promote the score-trajectory figure to the main text, next to Eq. (3)–(4).
  3. Add one plain-language paragraph before Eq. (3). Suggested text:
     > *In words: for every bad calibration cell we record the highest score it ever reached at any checkpoint before it failed or reached $L^\ast$. The pass threshold is set just above the scores that 90% of these bad cells never exceeded at any checkpoint (for $\alpha=0.10$). A new bad cell therefore has at most a 10% chance of ever crossing it, however often we look. The reject threshold is built in the same way from the lowest score each good calibration cell ever reached.*

**R9-04 (MAJOR): The abstract is 250 words, contains 14 numbers, and reports the calibration-time saving selectively.**
- *Location:* MS l.13.
- *Evidence:*
  - "calibration campaigns ended 2.61 times sooner than with full-lifetime calibration" is the MATR/CLO figure only. HUST was 1.38 (l.184), and total calibration cell-cycles fell by only 1.22 and 1.09.
  - "parametric and point-prediction rules violated them on one data set" is vague.
  - `wc -w` on the abstract returns 250 words. *Battery Energy*'s limit could not be verified (the guideline page returned 403; see journal_selection/selected_journal.md). Many Wiley energy titles cap abstracts at 200 words. UNVERIFIED.
- *Fix:* Proposed abstract (about 190 words):
  > Early-cycle lifetime predictions are increasingly used to stop cycle-life tests early, but the error rate of the resulting pass/fail decisions is rarely controlled, and re-checking a prediction as cycling proceeds inflates it. We present a sequential screening rule that, at a series of early checkpoints, passes a cell, rejects it or keeps it on test. Its pass and reject thresholds are set from the highest and lowest normalised model scores that held-out calibration cells reached at any checkpoint. This guarantees, without distributional assumptions, that a cell missing a cycle-life specification is passed, and a cell meeting it is rejected, with at most user-chosen probabilities, however often the engineer looks. Calibration cells need to be cycled only to the specification, not to end of life. On 246 LiFePO~4~/graphite cells from two laboratories (1,000 random splits; nominal error 0.10), false-pass and false-reject rates were 0.068–0.080 while 78% (MATR/CLO) and 51% (HUST) of the cycling was saved. Applying individually valid thresholds sequentially exceeded the target by up to 2.9 times. Under batch or laboratory shift the guarantee held only after recalibration on cells of the new population. The rule works with any lifetime predictor.

## B. Notation and terminology

**R9-08 (MAJOR): Checkpoint indexing switches between m and k across the text and figures.**
- *Evidence:*
  - Eq. (2) uses $h_m$, $s_m$, $S^{(m)}$, $X^{(m)}$ and checkpoint cycles $k_m$.
  - The text and Figure 2 caption use $s_k$ (l.122, l.124).
  - Figure 2 legend: "Score scale s_k" (literal underscore).
  - The workflow figure uses $S_k$, $h_k$, $s_k$.
  - Figure S1 and S3 axes: "Normalised score S_k". In those figures k means a cycle number, whereas in the equations $k_m$ is the cycle of checkpoint *m*.
- *Fix:* Use one convention throughout. Recommended: index by cycle number, $S_i(k)$, $h_k$, $s_k$, for $k\in\mathcal K=\{k_1,\dots,k_M\}$; engineers think in cycles. Then re-render all figure labels with mathtext ($s_k$, $S_k$).

**R9-09 (MINOR): The symbol P is used for two different things, and the pass/reject threshold naming is hard to follow.**
- *Evidence:*
  - $\mathrm P(\cdot)$ denotes probability (Eq. 1, Proposition 1).
  - $P_i$ denotes the bad-cell trajectory statistic (Eq. 3), so the proof contains "$\mathrm{P}(P_{\text{new}}>P_{(r_0)})$".
  - The reject rule is "$S<-\hat\mu$" with $\hat\mu$ built from $\max(-S)$. This double negative is correct but needs a second reading.
- *Fix:* Rename the trajectory statistics $U_i$ (upper excursion) and $D_i$ (lower excursion), or $M^+_i$ and $M^-_i$. Define the reject threshold directly as $\hat\tau_{\rm rej}=-\hat\mu$ so the rule reads "pass if $S>\hat\tau_{\rm pass}$, reject if $S<\hat\tau_{\rm rej}$".

**R9-10 (MINOR): The data-set name, the rule names and the terms for "time" vary across text, tables and figures.**
- *Evidence:*
  - Data set: "MATR/CLO" in text; "MATR+CLO" in Tables 2–3 and all figures; "matr_clo" in raw files.
  - Spelling: "data set" (l.13, l.100) and "dataset" (l.27, l.113) both appear.
  - Rule names: "Proposed (trajectory-calibrated)" (Table 2), "Proposed" (figures), "traj_unnorm", "gauss", "point_k100", "single_k100" (SI tables), "the procedure", "the rule", "the screening layer".
  - The phrase "cycles of wall-clock time" (l.229) mixes units: a cycle count is not a wall-clock time.
- *Fix:*
  - Fix one label per data set ("MATR/CLO") and one spelling ("data set").
  - Fix one short name per rule and use it in text, tables, figures and SI.
  - Write "campaign duration (cycles of the longest-running calibration cell)".

**R9-11 (MINOR): Statistical and ML jargon is used without a gloss for this readership.**
- *Evidence:*
  - "exchangeable" (first used at l.26, never explained).
  - "conformal" (in rule names, never explained).
  - "marginal", "class-conditional", "training-conditional (PAC)".
  - "out-of-fold", "matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation", "Monte-Carlo error", "optional-stopping".
  - "decades" (l.222) for log10 units.
