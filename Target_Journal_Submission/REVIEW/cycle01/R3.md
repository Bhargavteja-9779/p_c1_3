# R3 — Experimental design and evaluation (review cycle 1)

Reviewer lens: leakage, split construction, baseline fairness, the definitions of "time saved" and "early", the E4/E5/E9/E10 designs, number of splits, and whether the claims in Results 4.2–4.8 follow from the tables. I recomputed every number I cite below from `results/raw/*.csv(.gz)` and `data/features/*.npz`, using short pandas scripts run from the repository root. Line numbers refer to `manuscript/Draft_Manuscript.md` unless stated otherwise.

## What I verified and found sound

* **No leakage in the base predictors or the score scale.**
  * Per-checkpoint models are fitted on training cells only (`runner.py:41-58`). The imputer and scaler are fitted inside the sklearn pipeline on training cells (`models/base.py:23-24, 38`).
  * $s_k$ is the out-of-fold RMSE taken from `ElasticNetCV.mse_path_` on training cells (`base.py:39-42`), or from out-of-fold predictions on training cells for GBR and RF (`base.py:54-55`).
  * The noise SD in E4 comes from the training-feature SD (`runner.py:57, 75`).
  * Calibration cells enter only through `calibrate()` / `interval_calibrate()`.
  * No alive cell has NaN features in either `.npz`, so the imputer is never used on test rows.
  * A CNN scale picked as the minimum validation RMSE over epochs (`cnn.py:61-63`) is optimistic, but the trajectory calibration absorbs this.
* **The E1 split is correct for Proposition 1.** Training cells are drawn within dataset×class strata. Calibration and test cells are then a uniform random partition of the remaining pool (`runner.py:17-31`), so within each class they are exchangeable.
* **Seeds do not overlap.** E1 uses 20261115–20262114, E2 starts at 20361115 and so on; the pilot used seeds 1000–1099.
* **Time saved and early are computed the same way for every rule.** All rules go through the same `simulate()` / `metrics()` code (`procedure.py`) on the same test cells. A cell that fails before a checkpoint is rejected at time $T$. An undecided cell is charged $\min(T,L^\ast)$. "Early" is the `by_model` flag.
* **Table 2 reproduces exactly.** Examples:
  * MATR/CLO, proposed: FPR 0.0676, FRR 0.0803, saved 0.7828, early 0.8673, 141.5 cycles.
  * HUST, proposed: 0.0682 / 0.0736 / 0.5066 / 0.6052 / 857.4.
  * Naive: 0.1449 / 0.1579 (MATR/CLO) and 0.2857 / 0.2137 (HUST).
* **The other cited figures also reproduce:**
  * 26% / 21% of E1 splits with FPR > 0.10.
  * Calibration wall-clock 750 vs 1,958.1 cycles (factor 2.61).
  * Median decision time 40 cycles; 78.4% of cells decided by cycle 100.
  * E2 ranges (79.8–80.5% saved on MATR/CLO; 40.7–54.6% on HUST).
  * E3 grid numbers (84.3, 79.4, 70.8, 61.5, 52.6, 56.8, 40.6, 34.8, 81.9, 52.4).
  * E4 numbers (41.1, 80.2, 19.7, 51.9, 70.4, 37.4, 36.0, 17.3, 0.126/0.163, 0.275/0.187, 0.107/0.084, 0.139/0.139).
  * E7 numbers (0.034/0.025, 53.8, 94.2, 69.0, 69.7–79.4, 15.5/20.0, 5.6/5.8 cells).
  * E9 numbers (0.073 / 0.088, upper 0.087 / 0.101, 75.1%, 78 groups).
  * The analytic value 39% for $n=20$: Beta$(19,2)$ CDF at 0.9 = 0.392.

The problems are in how the experiments are framed and how fair the baselines are, not in the arithmetic.

## Findings

### R3-01 — MAJOR — "Laboratory shift breaks the guarantee without recalibration" is never tested
**Location:** Abstract (l.13): "Under batch and laboratory shift the guarantees failed unless thresholds were recalibrated on cells of the new population". Also §4.6 (l.206: "Across laboratories the picture was the same") and §5 (iii) (l.235).

**Evidence:** The cross-lab arm of E5 has only two scenarios, `source_model_target_cal` and `target_model_target_cal` (`suite.py:189`). Both calibrate on HUST cells, and there is no source-calibrated cross-lab scenario. From `E5_shift_splits.csv`:
* cross-lab, proposed rule: FPR 0.080 / FRR 0.070 in both scenarios.
* No cross-lab row has calibration on MATR/CLO cells.

So the data show that recalibration works across laboratories. They do not show that the guarantee fails across laboratories without it.

**Fix:** Either add a `source_model_source_cal` cross-lab scenario (MATR/CLO training and calibration, HUST test), or limit the abstract and Discussion claim to batch shift.

### R3-02 — MAJOR — Batch-shift results rest on 1–6 bad test cells; "restored validity" is trivial; "every rule failed" is false
**Location:** §4.6 l.206; Figure 7; Table S7.

**Evidence:** The test set is the same in every split of a scenario (`n_test` takes one value per scenario). Bad cells in the test set, from `E5_shift_splits.csv`:

| Target | Scenario | Bad test cells | Bad calibration cells | Pass possible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| b3 | source_cal | 3 (of 40) | | |
| b3 | target_cal, $n_t=20$ | 1 | 2 | Never (`pass_thr = inf` in 100% of splits) |
| b3 | target_cal, $n_t=30$ | 1 | 2 | Never |
| b4 (CLO) | target_cal, $n_t=20$ | 10 | 8 (< 9) | Never (100% `inf`) |
| b4 (CLO) | target_cal, $n_t=30$ | 6 | 12 | Yes |

Consequences:
* **(a)** "False-pass 0.128 (batch 3)" is an average over 200 splits of the same 3 bad cells. The 95% CIs in Figure 7 and Table S7 reflect only resampling of training and calibration cells, not test-population uncertainty. With 3 cells this is statistically empty.
* **(b)** "Recalibrating on target cells restored validity" is guaranteed on the false-pass side in 3 of the 4 target_cal arms, because the rule cannot pass at all. The text admits this only for batch 3, not for CLO with $n_t=20$.
* **(c)** "Every rule failed on later batches" is false. On b3 with source calibration, `point_k100` had FPR 0.055 and FRR 0.075, inside both targets.
* **(d)** Source_cal and target_cal arms use different test sets (target_cal removes the calibration cells), so their savings cannot be compared directly.

**Fix:**
* Report the number of bad and good test cells for each arm, and flag arms where passing is impossible.
* Replace split-level CIs with exact binomial CIs on the pooled test cells, or a cell-level bootstrap.
* Drop or reword the b3 false-pass claims.
* Correct "every rule failed".
* Consider a target with enough bad cells, e.g. a different $L^\ast$ for b3, or b3+b4 pooled.

### R3-03 — MAJOR — Confidence intervals understate uncertainty, and one headline violation is not significant at the cell level
**Location:** §3.6 l.113; Table 2 "FPR upper / FRR upper"; §4.2 l.154; §5 l.233; Abstract ("parametric … rules violated them on one data set").

**Evidence:** The reported CIs are Monte-Carlo CIs over 1,000 heavily overlapping splits of only 169 and 77 cells (81 and 40 bad cells). A cell-level bootstrap of the per-cell error frequencies (2,000 resamples, `E1_*_cells.csv.gz`) gives:

| Data set | Rule | FPR (95% CI) | FRR (95% CI) | Paper's upper limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MATR/CLO | proposed | 0.066 (0.033–0.102) | 0.079 (0.045–0.118) | 0.073 / 0.085 |
| HUST | proposed | 0.069 (0.044–0.095) | 0.074 (0.036–0.119) | |
| HUST | gauss | 0.081 (0.049–0.117) | 0.136 (0.072–0.203) | 0.143 |
| HUST | naive | 0.285 (0.191–0.388) | 0.210 (0.122–0.308) | |
| HUST | point_k100 | 0.237 (0.151–0.331) | 0.365 (0.247–0.486) | |

* The population-level CI of the proposed rule is about 5× wider than the one reported.
* On HUST, the "violation" by the parametric rule (FRR 0.136) is not distinguishable from 0.10 once cell sampling is taken into account. This matters because it is the only E1 evidence for "Model-based uncertainty is not a substitute" (§5) and for the abstract claim.
* The naive and point-rule violations on HUST stay clear.

**Fix:**
* Label the split-level CI as Monte-Carlo error only.
* Add a cell-level (cluster) bootstrap CI for every error rate in Tables 2, S3, S7, S10 and S11.
* Rest the "parametric rule is unsafe" argument on the cases that remain significant: GP on HUST, 0.274 / 0.362 in E2; HUST at $L^\ast$ = 1,600, FPR 0.322 in E7. Otherwise soften it.

### R3-04 — MAJOR — The parametric baseline has no multiplicity correction, so its failure is partly the repeated-looks effect
**Location:** §3.4 l.96; `runner.py:98`; §4.2 l.154; §5 l.233.

**Evidence:**
* `gauss` applies $z_{1-\alpha}$ and $-z_{1-\beta}$ at every one of the 16–17 checkpoints, with no correction for repeated looks. It is a "naive sequential" parametric rule. Its HUST FRR excess (0.136) can come from repeated looks as well as from model miscalibration, yet §5 credits it entirely to the model ("inherited the calibration of each model").
* $s_m$ is the CV-minimised RMSE (`base.py:39-42`, minimum over the penalty grid), which is optimistically small. This makes the Gaussian rule more liberal.
* On MATR/CLO the parametric rule is valid (0.046 / 0.043) and as efficient as the proposed rule: saved difference +0.8 pp, CI −1.4 to 3.2. Table 3 also shows it is faster for most cells (Wilcoxon Holm $p$=0.0015, $r$=+0.31, where positive means the proposed rule is slower). The text never discusses this, and the sign of $r$ disagrees with the sign of the saved difference.

**Fix:**
* Add a multiplicity-aware parametric comparator: Bonferroni-Gaussian $z_{1-\alpha/M}$, or a Gaussian max-statistic threshold using the OOF residual correlation across checkpoints.
* Rename the current rule "Gaussian threshold applied at every look".
* State plainly that on MATR/CLO a valid parametric rule matched the proposed rule.

### R3-05 — MAJOR — The lifetime-interval baseline targets a stricter goal, which inflates the reported advantage
**Location:** §3.4 l.96; `calibration.py:296-303`; Table 3 (+24.0 / +31.2 pp); §5 l.229.

**Evidence:**
* `interval_traj` calibrates one-sided normalised residuals over all calibration cells (both classes) at level $\alpha$. This bounds marginal miscoverage over all cells, not the class-conditional false-pass rate.
* Its realised rates (0.017 / 0.010 on MATR/CLO; 0.007 / 0.011 on HUST) are 5–10× below target. The 24–31 pp efficiency gap therefore mixes two effects: calibrating on the class instead of the lifetime, and spending far less of the error budget.
* §5 explains the gap with "its marginal guarantee covers both tails". That is inaccurate: each bound is one-sided at $\alpha$. The real cause is that residuals of cells far from $L^\ast$ dominate the quantile.

**Fix:**
* Add a class-conditional (Mondrian) interval comparator: lower-bound residuals from bad calibration cells only, upper-bound residuals from good cells only, still needing full lifetimes. This isolates the effect of truncation.
* Alternatively, also report the interval rule tuned to the same realised error, e.g. by matching FPR/FRR ≈ 0.07.
* Correct the explanation in §5.

### R3-06 — MAJOR — "Calibration campaigns ended 2.61 times sooner" leaves out training cells cycled to end of life
**Location:** Abstract; §4.3 l.184; §5 l.229.

**Evidence:**
* The elastic-net predictor is trained on full lifetimes. In E1 the training wall-clock is 2,130 cycles (MATR/CLO) and 2,656 cycles (HUST) per split (`train_wallclock`).
* Counting the whole preparatory campaign (training and calibration):

| | Proposed | Interval | Factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall-clock, max(train, cal), MATR/CLO | 2,130 | 2,206 | 1.04 |
| Wall-clock, max(train, cal), HUST | 2,656 | 2,683 | 1.01 |
| Cell-cycles incl. training, MATR/CLO | | | 1.06 |
| Cell-cycles incl. training, HUST | | | 1.04 |

* The 2.61× gain only appears if training cells are historical data obtained at no cost. The one fully truncated pipeline (logistic) saves 67.5% instead of 78.3% on MATR/CLO and 40.0% instead of 50.7% on HUST.

**Fix:**
* In the abstract and §4.3, state that the factor applies to calibration cells only and assumes existing full-lifetime training data.
* Report the whole-campaign factor next to it.
* Make the logistic pipeline the headline for a "fully truncated" campaign.

### R3-07 — MAJOR — The E10 (PAC) evaluation cannot check a training-conditional guarantee, and unfavourable numbers are left out
**Location:** §4.5 l.200; Table S11; `suite.py:309-326`; `calibration.py:227-242`.

**Evidence:**
* The pass/fail metric is "share of splits whose realised test FPR exceeds 0.10". That event mixes conditional error with binomial noise from the test sample:
  * MATR/CLO default has about 20 bad test cells, and HUST large_cal about 7.8. On HUST large_cal a single false pass already gives a rate above 0.10.
  * At a conditional error of 0.068, P(Bin(20, 0.068) ≥ 3) = 0.15. So this metric neither confirms nor refutes the PAC property, nor the "consistent with 39%" claim for the marginal rule.
* Omitted results:
  * MATR/CLO default: PAC never passes a cell in 75.0% of splits (`pass_thr = inf`).
  * MATR/CLO large_cal: PAC exceeds the target in 11.0% (FPR) and 11.5% (FRR) of splits, above $\delta = 0.10$ by the paper's own metric.
* The large_cal design also cuts the training fraction (0.50 → 0.35 on MATR/CLO, 0.40 → 0.30 on HUST), which confounds the effect of calibration size.
* PAC is cited as "(Section 3.3)", but Section 3.3 never defines it. The rank rule in `pac_rank` and $\delta = 0.1$ are hard-coded and not described in Methods.

**Fix:**
* Define the PAC rank in Methods.
* Evaluate conditional error properly: for each split, estimate it from the per-cell trajectory statistics of all cells not used for training. Or use a semi-synthetic resampling design with large test sets.
* Report the never-pass shares and the large_cal exceedances.
* Keep the training fraction fixed when varying calibration size.

### R3-08 — MAJOR — The E4 noise model (i.i.d. across checkpoints, at feature level) favours the paper's "sequential degrades faster" conclusion
**Location:** §4.6 l.208; §5 l.235; Figure 6; `runner.py:61-78`.

**Evidence:**
* Noise is drawn independently for every feature and every checkpoint, with an SD equal to a multiple of the between-cell training-feature SD. Real measurement errors (offsets, temperature, channel calibration) are strongly correlated across checkpoints. Features such as `q2`, `chargetime5` and `fade_intercept` are almost the same quantity at every checkpoint.
* Independent noise at each look maximises the "many chances to cross" effect behind the claim that the proposed rule degrades faster than the single-checkpoint rule (0.126 / 0.163 vs 0.107 / 0.084 at 0.25 SD on MATR/CLO).
* The noise levels are also not tied to any measured repeatability. 1 SD of between-cell spread removes most of the signal.
* None of this design is described in Methods.

**Fix:**
* Add a correlated noise scenario: a cell-level offset shared by all checkpoints, or noise injected into $Q(V)$ / capacity and passed through feature extraction.
* Calibrate noise levels to the repeatability of the instrument.
* Describe the noise model in §3.6.
* Make the practical recommendation conditional on the noise structure.

### R3-09 — MAJOR — Methods do not describe the designs of E3, E4, E5, E9 and E10, and post-freeze status appears only in the SI
**Location:** §3.6 l.113 describes only the E1 split.

**Evidence:** None of the following appears in the main text; they exist only in `suite.py`:
* the batch-shift split (60% of b1+b2 for training, 40% for source calibration, class-stratified target calibration of 20 or 30 cells);
* the cross-lab capacity-only feature set;
* the protocol-group assignment (coin flip per group, giving test sizes of 11–64);
* the noise model;
* the PAC rank;
* the large_cal fractions.

That E9 and E10 were added after the design freeze is stated only in SI §S1. Also, §3.6 says other experiments use "200–333" splits, but the CNN uses 30 splits, and 333 is never used (`n_splits_secondary: 200`).

**Fix:**
* Add a short design paragraph per experiment to §3.6, or a design table in the main text.
* Mark E9 and E10 as post-hoc in the main text.
* Correct the split counts.

### R3-10 — MINOR — The conservativeness is mostly discreteness, not the maximum statistic
**Location:** §4.2 l.152: "The rates are below the nominal 0.10 because the maximum statistic is conservative".

**Evidence:** The exact finite-sample bound $(n_0+1-r_0)/(n_0+1)$, averaged over the realised calibration sizes, is:
* MATR/CLO: 0.081 (false pass) and 0.084 (false reject).
* HUST: 0.076 and 0.078.

The single-checkpoint rule reaches 0.082 / 0.060 (MATR/CLO) and 0.071 / 0.079 (HUST). So the maximum statistic costs about 1.3 pp on MATR/CLO and about 0.8 pp on HUST. The remaining gap to 0.10 is the discreteness of the order statistic.

**Fix:** Report the exact attainable level next to the nominal 0.10 in Table 2, and correct the explanation.

### R3-11 — MINOR — Bonferroni cannot decide anything here, yet it is tested as if it were a competitor
**Location:** Table 2 and Table 3 (Bonferroni rows, $p$ = 1.2e-28).

**Evidence:**
* With about 20 calibration cells per class, $\hat\lambda = +\infty$ by construction (Remark ii). Its rows (accuracy 1.000, 0% saved) and Wilcoxon $p$-values say nothing beyond the Remark.
* In E3 it is not run on the sparse 4-checkpoint grid, where it would need only $n_0 \ge 39$ and could become feasible on HUST large_cal or MATR/CLO with 45% calibration.

**Fix:** Report Bonferroni as "infeasible (no finite threshold)" rather than testing it. Optionally run it on a sparse grid with the E10 large calibration set, so that a multiplicity-correcting comparator can actually decide.

### R3-12 — MINOR — Inconsistent inference in Table 3
**Evidence:**
* MATR/CLO point_k100: the bootstrap CI of the saved difference (−9.3 to −3.5) excludes 0, but the Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon $p$ = 0.16.
* MATR/CLO gauss: the saved difference is +0.8 (favours the proposed rule), but $r$ = +0.31 with $p$ = 0.0015 (favours the comparator).

The two statistics have different estimands: a ratio of sums versus the median of per-cell differences. The text reads them as if they agreed.

**Fix:** Pick one primary estimand and test, or explain the difference. Then rewrite "the rules that saved more time … did so by violating at least one error target". On MATR/CLO the point rule (FPR 0.079 / FRR 0.073, cell CI to 0.139 / 0.12) and the parametric rule were both empirically valid and at least as fast.

### R3-13 — MINOR — The same quantity is reported with two values
**Evidence:**
* §4.2 says 26% / 21% of splits exceed 0.10 (from E1).
* §4.5 says 28% / 24% (from E10 default, 200 splits: 28.5% / 24.5%).
* §4.5 compares PAC against "78.5%" (E10), while Table 2 gives 78.3%.
* §4.3 uses 1,739 cycles (per-cell average) while Table 2 gives 1,737.

**Fix:** Quote E1 values for the marginal rule throughout and label E10 values as such.

### R3-14 — MINOR — E9 is not covered by Proposition 1
**Location:** §4.6 l.206 ("since calibration and test cells still came from the same protocol population").

**Evidence:**
* Groups are assigned to calibration or test by a coin flip (`suite.py:298`), so cells are exchangeable only at the group level (78 groups, 1–5+ cells each). Calibration size varies from 8 to 38 bad cells, and test size from 11 to 64 cells.
* FRR 0.088 with upper limit 0.101 is above the exact attainable bound (about 0.08).

**Fix:** State that E9 tests robustness to clustering beyond the proposition. Consider a group-level calibration statistic, and report a group-bootstrap CI.

### R3-15 — MINOR — Feature and split design were changed after looking at outcome data
**Location:** §3.2 l.66; §3.6 l.113; `EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` 16:00 and 16:42.

**Evidence:**
* The 5-cycle median feature fix was chosen after inspecting feature–lifetime correlations and MAPE on all cells, including future calibration and test cells.
* The split construction was changed after the first full E1 run showed single_k100 FPR 0.101.
* Both changes are well motivated, and the second is disclosed. Still, "fixed after a 100-split pilot used only to detect data defects" understates this: the pilot computed all methods' error rates (log 16:02, 16:05).

**Fix:** Reword §3.6 to say exactly what was looked at before the design was frozen.

### R3-16 — MINOR — Too few CNN splits, and the "saved" reference point flatters the headline
**Evidence:**
* The CNN uses 30 splits; its HUST FRR CI runs from 0.060 to 0.166.
* The headline "78.3% saved" is relative to running every cell to $\min(T,L^\ast)$. Against the standard practice of deciding at cycle 100 (the point rule), the proposed rule saves 6.4 pp *less* on MATR/CLO.

**Fix:** Use at least 200 CNN splits, or drop the CNN. Report savings against the cycle-100 point rule in the abstract or §4.3 as well.

## Overall recommendation: **major revision**

The core validity result is sound and reproduces exactly. I found no leakage: features, $s_k$ and the hyperparameters come from training cells only, and the E1 split matches the theorem. However:
* the shift, noise and PAC experiments are designed or reported in ways that do not support the conclusions drawn from them;
* the comparators are tilted: the parametric rule has no multiplicity correction, and the interval rule spends far less of its error budget than its target allows;
* the abstract contains a claim with no experiment behind it (laboratory shift without recalibration) and an efficiency factor that leaves out the cost of the training campaign.

### The three changes that matter most
1. **Fix the claims about shift:** run a cross-lab scenario with source calibration, or drop the claim (R3-01); report test-cell counts and cell-level CIs for E5, and correct "every rule failed" and "restored validity" (R3-02).
2. **Make the baselines fair and the inference cell-level:** add a multiplicity-aware parametric rule and a class-conditional interval rule (R3-04, R3-05); report cell-bootstrap CIs, under which the HUST parametric "violation" is not significant (R3-03).
3. **Qualify the efficiency claims:** state the whole-campaign factor of about 1.04 next to the 2.61× calibration factor (R3-06), and redo the PAC evaluation (R3-07) and the noise model (R3-08) so they test what they claim.
