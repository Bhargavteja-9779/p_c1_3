## Findings

### R6-01 — MAJOR — "Under batch and laboratory shift the guarantees failed unless thresholds were recalibrated": the laboratory half was never tested
* Locations: Abstract (DM:13); §4.6 "Across laboratories the picture was the same" (DM:206); Discussion (iii) "it failed under batch and laboratory shift in our experiments unless a few target cells were used for recalibration" (DM:235); Limitations (DM:242).
* Evidence: `E5_shift_splits.csv` contains only two cross-lab scenarios, `source_model_target_cal` and `target_model_target_cal`. Both calibrate on HUST cells. No cross-lab scenario calibrates on source cells, so no experiment shows the guarantee failing under laboratory shift. The text's own result is that the cross-lab rule *was valid* (0.080/0.070).
* Fix: either add a MATR/CLO-calibrated → HUST-tested scenario, or rewrite these sentences to say "batch shift (laboratory shift was tested only with target recalibration)".

### R6-02 — MAJOR — "With model and calibration from MATR batches 1 and 2, every rule failed on later batches" is false
* Location: §4.6, DM:206. Figure 7 reinforces the claim.
* Evidence (Table S7): b3 source_cal `point_k100` has FPR 0.055 (upper limit 0.072) and FRR 0.0746 (upper limit 0.0865), so it is within both targets. The point rule is also absent from Figure 7 (`Fig10_distribution_shift.png` shows only Proposed, Single, Parametric and Naive), which hides the one counter-example.
* Fix: "every rule except the point-prediction rule on batch 3 failed …", and add point_k100 to Figure 7 or state why it is omitted.

### R6-03 — MAJOR — Batch-3 shift rates rest on 1–3 fixed test cells, and the CIs do not reflect this
* Location: §4.6 (DM:206); Table S7; Figure 7.
* Evidence: in `E5_shift_splits.csv` the number of bad test cells per split has std 0. It is 3 for b3 source_cal and 1 for b3 target_cal (n = 20 and 30), so the same cells appear in every split. The b3 source_cal FPR values are {0, 1/3, 2/3} (125/73/2 splits). The reported "0.128 (CI 0.104–0.152)" is therefore Monte-Carlo variation over training draws for three specific cells, not a population false-pass rate. "Recalibration restored validity" on b3 (FPR = 0.000) is vacuous, because with about 2 bad calibration cells passing is impossible (the text notes this) and only one bad test cell is ever evaluated. The b4 rates rest on 18, 10 and 6 fixed bad cells.
* Fix: report n_bad and n_good per scenario in Table S7 and in the caption of Figure 7. Drop the CI or label it "over training splits; test cells fixed". Do not count b3 as evidence of restored validity.

### R6-04 — MAJOR — The claim about rules that saved more time omits a valid rule that was significantly faster per cell
* Location: §4.3, DM:182: "The rules that saved more time than the proposed one — naive sequential, point prediction and, on HUST, the parametric rule — did so by violating at least one error target on at least one data set." Also "Among the valid rules, the proposed rule saved more …".
* Evidence (Table 3 and my recomputation):
  * MATR/CLO parametric rule: it was valid on MATR/CLO (0.046/0.043). Its mean saving difference is +0.8 points (CI −1.4 to 3.2), but the Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon p = 0.0015 with r = +0.31 *favours the parametric rule*: 70.4% of cells were decided faster by it (median per-cell time difference +7.5 cycles against the proposed rule). Table 3 thus shows a positive "Saved diff." next to a positive r of opposite meaning, and the text never explains the discrepancy.
  * MATR/CLO point rule: it was valid on MATR/CLO (upper limits 0.083/0.076) and saved 6.4 points more (CI −9.3 to −3.5, which excludes 0). It violated targets only on HUST, so "on at least one data set" carries the whole argument.
  * The single-checkpoint rule also saved more on MATR/CLO (−1.3, not significant).
* Fix: state plainly that on MATR/CLO the point and parametric rules were empirically valid and at least as fast, and that the argument for the proposed rule is the *guarantee* (they failed on HUST or with other models), not efficiency. Explain the mean-versus-median sign conflict in the parametric row.

### R6-05 — MAJOR — "Abstract: parametric and point-prediction rules violated them on one data set" understates the parametric rule's failures; and the calibration speed-up quoted is MATR/CLO only
* Location: Abstract (DM:13).
* Evidence: Table S3 shows the parametric rule exceeding 0.10 on *both* data sets for some predictors: MATR/CLO GP FRR 0.104; HUST GBR FRR 0.114, CNN 0.183/0.25, GP 0.274/0.362. E7 HUST L* = 1600 gives 0.322, and E4 noise_both reaches up to 0.391. Separately, "calibration campaigns ended 2.61 times sooner" is the MATR/CLO factor. The HUST factor is 1.38, and cell-cycles fell by only 1.22 and 1.09.
* Fix: "with the elastic net … violated them on one data set, and with other base predictors on both". Quote "2.61 and 1.38 times sooner (MATR/CLO, HUST)".

### R6-06 — MAJOR — The training-conditional (PAC) variant is used and cross-referenced but never defined
* Location: §4.5 "The training-conditional (PAC) variant with δ = 0.10 (Section 3.3)" (DM:200); Discussion (DM:235); Limitations (DM:243); Table S11/S12.
* Evidence: Section 3.3 (DM:68–92) contains no PAC rule. `grep PAC` finds no definition anywhere in the DM or the SI; only the caption of Table S12 hints at "the rank required for a PAC guarantee". Results such as 28.1%, 56.3%, "never reached a decision" and "needs at least 22" cannot be checked from the paper alone.
* Fix: add the PAC rank definition (the smallest r with Beta(r, n+1−r) CDF at 1−α ≤ δ) to §3.3 as a remark, together with its guarantee, and keep the cross-reference.

### R6-09 — MINOR — The same statistic is reported with two different values
* §4.2 (DM:152): the realised FPR exceeded 0.10 in **26%** (MATR/CLO) and **21%** (HUST) of splits (E1, 1,000 splits).
* §4.5 (DM:200): "In **28%** (MATR/CLO) and **24%** (HUST) of the splits the realised false-pass rate … exceeded 0.10" (E10 default, 200 splits). This is the same configuration under a different experiment ID, and the paper does not say so.
* §4.5 then calls 26–28% "consistent with the analytic probability … (39% for 20 calibration cells)". Those quantities differ: the realised rate on about 20 test cells includes test-sampling noise, and the max statistic is conservative. Observed 26–28% against 39% is not evidently "consistent".
* Fix: use the E1 numbers in both places, and replace "consistent with" by a correct statement, for example "of the same order; the analytic value refers to the conditional error probability, not the realised rate on ~20 test cells".

### R6-10 — MINOR — Some "valid" claims ignore CI upper limits above the nominal level
* E9 (DM:206): "remained valid (0.073 and 0.088; upper confidence limits 0.087 and **0.101**)". The FRR upper limit exceeds 0.10.
* §4.4 (DM:188): "The only point estimate above a nominal level, the false-reject rate of the CNN on HUST". By upper limits, MATR/CLO RF FRR (0.089, upper 0.101), MATR/CLO CNN (FPR upper 0.1005, FRR upper 0.117) and HUST CNN FPR (upper 0.105) are also above 0.10.
* §4.4 (DM:192): "Every grid kept the proposed rule within the targets". HUST single_100 FPR upper limit is 0.1007.
* §4.7 (DM:214): "The rule met its targets at every combination". HUST α = 0.05, β = 0.2 FRR is 0.186 with upper limit 0.209 > 0.2.
* These are Monte-Carlo-consistent with validity, but "valid" is asserted from point estimates.
* Fix: use the phrasing "point estimates below the nominal level; 95% CIs include it in N cases", and list those cases.

## Overall recommendation
**Major revision.** The headline numbers (abstract, Table 2, conclusions) are reproduced exactly from the raw data, and `build_numbers.py` computes correctly every key I checked. However, several qualitative claims about results are stronger than the data support:
* laboratory-shift failure was never tested;
* "every rule failed" under batch shift is false;
* the batch-3 shift rates come from 1–3 fixed cells;
* the efficiency comparison passes over valid, faster rules on MATR/CLO;
* the PAC rule is used but never defined.

## The 3 changes that matter most
1. Remove or substantiate the claims of laboratory-shift failure (R6-01) and "every rule failed" (R6-02). Report the fixed test-cell counts behind the batch-shift rates (R6-03).
2. Rewrite the efficiency comparison in §4.3 and the abstract honestly: on MATR/CLO the point and parametric rules were empirically valid and as fast or faster. The case for the proposed rule is the guarantee across data sets and models (R6-04, R6-05).
3. Define the PAC variant in Methods, and fix the wrong cross-references and the split counts (R6-06, R6-07, R6-08). Also report the 26%/28% split-exceedance figure once (R6-09) and qualify "valid" where CIs cross the nominal level (R6-10).
