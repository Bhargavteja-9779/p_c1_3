## Summary verdict on the novelty claim

The Gap paragraph claims that no existing method has all of (i) per-cell false-pass and false-reject control with finite-sample, distribution-free guarantees, (ii) validity under data-dependent stopping, and (iii) calibration without cycling to end of life. I found no battery paper that combines all three, so the claim survives as a claim about the battery domain.

Each ingredient on its own, however, is established, and several of them are uncited:

- **(i) + (iii).** These are properties of class-conditional (Mondrian / Neyman-Pearson) split-conformal classification of the binary label Z = 1{T ≥ L\*}. The paper's own single-checkpoint comparator already has both (Section 3.4: "valid, Proposition 1 with M=1"). Truncation at a cut-off is the device of Candès-Lei-Ren [34]. It is also used for screening "survival beyond a horizon" by Sesia & Svetnik (arXiv 2512.18118, 2025), which is not cited.
- **(ii).** A max-over-time score is the standard way to get simultaneous or any-time conformal validity:
  - Lindemann et al. (ICCPS 2023) and Cleaveland et al. (AAAI 2024) use it. Cleaveland et al.'s score is max(α₁R₁,…,α_T R_T), i.e. Eq. (3) with weights, where the paper fixes weight 1/s_m.
  - Conformal guarantees at data-dependent stopping times are the subject of SpikeCP (IEEE JSTSP 2024), Ringel et al. (ICML 2024) and, most closely, COINS (arXiv 2609.07112, Sep 2026). COINS is explicitly motivated by *sequential screening*, defines "any-stage validity", and compares against Bonferroni.

None of these are cited. Section 2 currently names only Jazbec et al. [38] as the sequential relative.

The novelty statement therefore **must be weakened and re-attributed**. The defensible contribution is:

1. the three-outcome, class-conditional formulation for cycle-life screening;
2. the observation that the screening guarantee needs only cells cycled to min(T, L\*);
3. a careful empirical demonstration on public battery data, including the size of the optional-stopping inflation in practice and failure under shift.

The defensible contribution is not a new statistical device. In my judgement that is still enough for *Battery Energy*, an applied battery journal, but only if it is framed honestly. Two more things are needed: the closest prior art must be cited and discussed, and the added value of the sequential element over a single well-chosen checkpoint must be shown against a non-strawman sequential competitor (R8-05).

## Findings

### R8-01: The closest methodological prior art for sequential validity is not cited (MAJOR)

**Location.** Section 2 "Statistical foundations" (Draft_Manuscript.md:38); Remark (ii) (:92); Discussion "Repeated looks must be paid for" (:227).

**Evidence.**
- Related Work cites only [17,18] (Wald; Howard et al.) and Jazbec et al. [38] for sequential validity.
- Remark (ii) states "The maximum statistic accounts for the dependence between looks exactly" without attribution.
- Uncited work that does this:
  - COINS (arXiv 2609.07112) treats "sequential screening" with any-stage validity and benchmarks against Bonferroni (table, item A).
  - SpikeCP (JSTSP 2024) gives conformal guarantees at input-dependent stopping times (item C).
  - Ringel et al. (ICML 2024) give a distribution-free calibrated stopping rule (item B).
  - Cleaveland et al. (AAAI 2024) and Lindemann et al. (ICCPS 2023) use exactly a (weighted) max-over-time score for simultaneous conformal validity (item D).
- A statistics-literate reviewer at *Battery Energy* will know at least Ringel/Romano and the max-score trick, and will read the current text as an overclaim.

**Fix.**
- Cite A-D in Section 2.
- Add one sentence to Remark (ii), e.g.: "Calibrating on the maximum of (weighted) scores over time is the standard route to simultaneous conformal validity [Lindemann 2023; Cleaveland 2024]; any-stage-valid sequential conformal sets were recently studied in generality by [COINS]. We use the simplest instance, with fixed weights 1/s_m, and show that it suffices for screening."
- In the Gap paragraph, change "its novelty lies in … the trajectory-wise calibration" to "its novelty lies in the screening formulation and in combining trajectory (max-score) calibration with class-conditional, truncated calibration".
- Note that COINS appeared in September 2026. If the authors argue it is concurrent, say so explicitly, but it must be cited.

### R8-02: Battery-specific conformal cycle-life work is omitted, and the Related Work misstates its extent (MAJOR)

**Location.** Introduction (:21), "conformal prediction intervals for lifetime [19–21]"; Related Work (:36), "has recently been applied to state-of-health forecasting [29,30]".

**Evidence.**
- [19–21] are generic textbooks and tutorials, not battery lifetime papers.
- Conformal intervals for early-cycle *cycle-life* prediction exist:
  - Wu et al., *Algorithms* 2026, 19, 535, on the same 124 MIT-Stanford cells used here, including a shift test;
  - Liu et al., *Energy Engineering* 2026, 123(8);
  - Pregowska & Marynowicz, arXiv 2604.16580;
  - for RUL, Javanmardi & Hüllermeier, IJPHM 2023;
  - calibrated cycle-life ranges from the first 100 cycles, Zhang et al., IEEE TTE 2023.
- A battery decision threshold calibrated conformally with bounded false-alarm risk already exists (Li, *Batteries* 2026, 12, 116).
- The literature matrix (literature/literature_matrix.md, section A) contains none of these. The prior-art search (literature/topic_selection.md §4) missed them.

**Fix.**
- Cite G, H, J (Zhang et al.) and K. Optionally cite I.
- Rewrite Related Work as: "Conformal prediction … has recently been applied to early cycle-life and RUL prediction [G,H,I,J] and SOH forecasting [29,30], and to calibrating early-warning thresholds [K]. All of these calibrate on cells observed to end of life, or report intervals at a fixed time, and none controls class-conditional screening errors under sequential stopping."
- Make the lifetime-interval comparator (Section 3.4) cite G as its battery precedent.

### R8-03: The truncated-calibration result and per-class control are attributed to the new procedure, but they come from classifying on Z (MAJOR)

**Location.** Abstract ("We prove that … calibration cells need to be cycled only to the specification"); contribution 2 (:26); Remark (i) (:92); Section 4.3 "Truncated calibration" (:183); Discussion "Truncated campaigns" (:229).

**Evidence.**
- The thresholds depend only on Z and on scores up to k_M < L\*.
- The single-checkpoint class-conditional rule (Section 3.4) has exactly the same property and is also valid ("as Proposition 1 (with M=1) … imply", :152).
- The 2.61× shorter calibration campaign (:183) is measured against the *lifetime-interval* rule. It is therefore a property of "classify Z with class-conditional (Mondrian / Neyman-Pearson) conformal thresholds" versus "regress T". It is not a property of the max-over-checkpoints calibration.
- The truncation idea, using min(T, c₀) so that only partial follow-up is needed, is that of Candès-Lei-Ren [34]. It is applied to horizon screening by Sesia & Svetnik (item E, uncited).
- Class-conditional (Mondrian) conformal prediction (Vovk et al. 2005, ch. 4; Vovk 2003; Lei 2014, "Classification with confidence", Biometrika) and the NP umbrella order statistic [32] give (i) directly.

**Fix.**
- In the abstract and contribution 2, separate the two results: "(a) because the thresholds are class-conditional, calibration needs only min(T, L\*) cycles (as in conformalised survival analysis [34]); (b) because they are calibrated on the maximum over checkpoints, they remain valid under any stopping time."
- Credit [34] and Sesia & Svetnik at Remark (i).
- Cite Mondrian conformal classification (Vovk 2003 / Vovk et al. 2005 ch. 4; Lei 2014) in Section 2.
- In Section 4.3, say that the single-checkpoint rule enjoys the same calibration-campaign reduction.

### R8-04: "Per-cell" in the Gap contradicts "class-conditional, not cell-conditional" in the Discussion (MAJOR)

**Location.** Gap (:40), "controls per-cell false-pass and false-reject probabilities"; Discussion (:235), "The guarantee is class-conditional, not cell-conditional".

**Evidence.** The guarantee is marginal over calibration and test cells and conditional only on class (Remark iii). "Per-cell" invites the reading that individual cells receive the guarantee, which the paper itself disclaims (:30, :235).

The distinction also matters for novelty. Existing battery conformal intervals (items G, H, J) already give a finite-sample, distribution-free bound on the *overall* misclassification probability of the rule "pass if lower bound ≥ L\*". The real increment is the *class-conditional* (two-type) control, not distribution-free error control per se.

**Fix.** Replace with "controls the class-conditional false-pass and false-reject probabilities (marginally over cells)". Add "existing conformal lifetime intervals control only the overall, not the class-wise, error of a pass/fail decision".

### R8-05: The added value of the sequential element is not convincingly shown, and the only valid sequential competitor is a strawman (MAJOR)

**Location.** Section 4.3, Table 3 (:162-177); Section 4.4 grid ablation (:190); Remark (ii) (:92).

**Evidence.**
- The novel element (ii) gives its benefit only through sequential looks, but:
  - On MATR/CLO the proposed rule versus the single-checkpoint (cycle 100) conformal rule: -1.3 points (95% CI -3.4 to 0.7, Holm p = 0.36).
  - On HUST, "the best single checkpoint (cycle 300, 61.5%) saved more than any sequential grid (dense 52.6%, sparse 56.8%)" (:190).
- The Bonferroni comparator cannot decide at all with the available calibration sizes, by the paper's own arithmetic: n₀ < 159 means λ̂ = +∞ (:92). So the "78.3 points saved versus Bonferroni" (Table 3, row 1) is guaranteed by construction and is not evidence.
- The prior art offers non-trivial valid sequential alternatives that a reviewer will ask for:
  - Simes / Holm-type corrections (SpikeCP);
  - weighted max with weights tuned on a split of the calibration data (Cleaveland et al.);
  - COINS-type stagewise allocation;
  - "choose the best single checkpoint on training cells (inner CV), then single-checkpoint conformal", which is also valid if the choice uses only training cells.
- Without at least the last one, the practical case for the trajectory calibration rests on not knowing the best checkpoint in advance (:190). That is a reasonable argument but it is not quantified.

**Fix.**
- Add the "training-selected single checkpoint" rule and one improved sequential correction (Simes/Holm, or a weighted max tuned on training cells) as comparators.
- Drop "versus Bonferroni" from headline efficiency claims, or say that Bonferroni is infeasible at these sample sizes.
- If the trajectory rule does not beat a training-selected checkpoint, reframe the contribution as robustness to checkpoint choice plus protection against the naive sequential practice. That is still a legitimate, publishable message.

### R8-06: The novelty of the formulation ignores three-way Neyman-Pearson classification with indecisions (MINOR)

**Location.** Contribution 1 (:25); Section 2 (:38).

**Evidence.** Three-outcome rules (decide good / decide bad / abstain) controlling both class-conditional error types are the subject of arXiv 2610.04233 (2026) and Ndaoud et al., arXiv 2412.12807. Sadinle et al. [33] is cited but is set-valued, which is the closest equivalent.

**Fix.** Cite both and note the difference. Here abstention means "keep cycling" with a later look, and the errors are unconditional rather than selective.

### R8-07: The engineering precedent of sequential life testing is not acknowledged (MINOR)

**Location.** Introduction (:21), "A second, less discussed problem is repeated looking".

**Evidence.**
- Sequential life tests with α/β control, for example SPRT-based reliability demonstration tests for exponential or Weibull lives (Epstein & Sobel 1955; MIL-HDBK-781 practice), are textbook reliability engineering.
- Battery patents (US 10,191,116; US 10,992,156; US 11,614,491) describe stopping tests when prediction confidence is high, without a guarantee (UNVERIFIED beyond abstracts).

**Fix.** Add one sentence citing a reliability-testing reference. Contrast: those methods are parametric and decide lots, whereas this procedure is distribution-free and decides individual cells from ML scores. Optionally mention the patent practice as motivation.

## Is the contribution enough for *Battery Energy*?

Conditionally yes. The statistical machinery is a direct assembly of known parts:
- class-conditional split conformal;
- a max-over-time score;
- a truncated outcome.

The proof of Proposition 1 is a five-line rank argument. On its own this would be thin for a statistics venue. For *Battery Energy* the value lies elsewhere:

1. It poses cycle-life screening as a two-error sequential decision.
2. It shows on two public datasets that the natural practice of re-checking predictions inflates error rates up to 2.9× (0.286 versus 0.10 on HUST).
3. It shows that calibration needs only cells cycled to L\*.
4. It honestly reports failure under batch and laboratory shift.

This is useful to battery-testing practitioners and is not available in the battery literature.

For that framing to survive review:
- the closest prior art (A-E, G, K) must be cited;
- the novelty statement must be re-attributed (R8-01, R8-03, R8-04);
- the advantage of the sequential element must be demonstrated against a fair competitor, or the contribution reframed (R8-05).
