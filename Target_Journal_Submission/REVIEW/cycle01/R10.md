# R10 — Skeptical handling editor / devil's advocate

Reviewer: R10. Lens: editor deciding desk-reject vs review, then the harshest plausible referee.
Files read: manuscript/Draft_Manuscript.md (all), tables/T2,T3,T5,T6,T7,T8,T10,T12, results/manuscript_numbers.json,
results/raw/E1_*_{splits,cells}.csv(.gz), code/src/batscreen/screening/calibration.py, evaluation/runner.py,
screening/procedure.py, journal_selection/selected_journal.md.

## Editor's desk verdict (first pass)

Send to review, not desk-reject: the problem (repeated looks at early predictions) is real, the
experimental record is large and honest in its body text, and the code is clean. But an editor reading
the title, abstract and conclusions and then Tables 2/3, S5, S6 and S7 sees a gap. The headline sells
**guaranteed** error rates and large savings. The body shows four things. (a) The guarantee is marginal, and
in about half of the simulated campaigns at least one realised error rate exceeds the target. (b) The
simplest valid competitor (one conformal checkpoint) is as good on MATR/CLO. On HUST it is beaten only
when it is put at a checkpoint that does not suit that dataset. (c) Under the realistic deployment
condition (new batch or lab, recalibrated) savings collapse to 6–27%, and the single-checkpoint rule saves
*more*. (d) The sequential rule is *more* fragile than the single-checkpoint rule to noise that is present
only at screening time. None of (a)–(d) is in the abstract or conclusions. A referee will find all of
them, so it is better to say them first.

## Answers to the lens questions (short)

* **Is the practical value real?** It is real in one place: it removes the optional-stopping inflation of
  ad-hoc re-checking (naive 0.145–0.286). The added value over a **valid single-checkpoint rule** is
  not shown. On MATR/CLO it is zero or negative. On HUST it is positive against cycle 100. In my
  training-only selection pilot (below) the margin shrinks to about 5 points, and it is negative against
  the best fixed checkpoint. Under shift with recalibration it is negative. Under test-only noise it is
  negative.
* **Is "guaranteed error rates" overclaimed?** Yes (R10-01).
* **Are the comparisons stacked?** Partly. The single checkpoint is fixed at Severson's cycle 100 for
  HUST. Bonferroni at M = 16/17 with about 20 calibration cells cannot decide by construction. The
  parametric rule uses no multiplicity correction. The interval rule is forced to use full-lifetime
  calibration. The cost metric switches between cell-cycles and wall-clock time to suit each claim.
  L\* sits at the median and α = 0.10 is lenient (R10-03, R10-04, R10-07).
* **Are the limitations honest?** The body text is mostly honest. The Limitations list and the abstract or
  conclusions are incomplete (R10-11).

## Findings

### R10-01 — MAJOR (borderline CRITICAL for the title) — "Guaranteed error rates" overclaims a marginal guarantee
*Location:* Title (line 1), running title (line 3), Abstract (line 13: "We prove that this bounds…"),
Conclusions (line 250: "the rule met its error targets").
*Evidence:* Proposition 1 bounds the error probability averaged over calibration and test draws. In E1 the
realised rates exceeded 0.10 often. False pass exceeded it in 26% (MATR/CLO) and 21% (HUST) of splits
(manuscript_numbers.json `E1.*.proposed.fpr_above`). False reject exceeded it in **31%** and **24%**
(`frr_above`), and the manuscript does not report these two numbers. In E10 with a larger calibration
set, HUST exceedance was **36%** (FPR) and **32%** (FRR) (`E10.h.large_cal.proposed.*_above`). I
recomputed the joint event from results/raw/E1_*_splits.csv. **At least one of the two rates exceeded
0.10 in 50.6% of MATR/CLO splits and 42.6% of HUST splits.**
```
MATR proposed: either exceed 0.506 ; HUST either exceed 0.426 (mean 12.1 bad / 10.9 good test cells)
```
Much of this comes from small test sets: each HUST split has about 12 bad test cells, so 2 false passes give 0.167. Still, to a lab
manager "guaranteed error rate" means "my campaign will not exceed 10%". That is false for roughly every
second campaign. The Discussion (line 235) explains this properly, but the title and abstract do not.
*Fix:* Replace "Guaranteed" with "distribution-free control of (marginal) error rates" (see the proposed titles
below). In the abstract, add one sentence: "The guarantee is marginal: in individual campaigns the realised
rates exceeded the target in 21–31% of splits per error type; a training-conditional variant removes this
but needs ≥22 calibration cells per class." Report FRR exceedance and the joint exceedance next to the FPR
exceedance at lines 152 and 200.

### R10-02 — MAJOR — The headline gives no gain over the simplest valid alternative (a single conformal checkpoint), and the abstract does not say so
*Location:* Abstract; Section 4.3 (line 182); Discussion "One checkpoint or many?" (line 231); Conclusions.
*Evidence:*
* E1 MATR/CLO: single-checkpoint conformal saved 79.6% and the proposed rule 78.3% (Table 2). The
  cell-level difference is −1.3 points (CI −3.4 to 0.7, Table 3).
* E4 (T7): on MATR/CLO the single-checkpoint rule **matches or beats** the proposed rule at every
  calibration fraction (41.5/73.9/76.1/80.2 vs 41.1/71.1/72.1/80.2) and every training fraction
  (77.2/78.1/79.4/80.2 vs 70.4/75.3/79.4/80.2).
* E7 (T10): on MATR/CLO single ≥ proposed at α = 0.05 (57.2 vs 53.8), L\* = 550 (77.6 vs 77.5) and L\* = 750
  (80.0 vs 79.4). The proposed rule wins only at L\* = 900 (+2.9) and α = 0.20 (+9.5).
* E3 (T6): on HUST the best single checkpoint (cycle 300) saved 61.5%, against 52.6% for the default dense grid and
  56.8% for the best sequential grid.
* On MATR/CLO even the uncalibrated Severson point rule stayed within target (0.079/0.073) and saved 84.7% (Table 2).

The abstract and conclusions report only the comparisons the proposed rule wins: naive sequential,
lifetime interval, and Bonferroni.
*Fix:* Add to the abstract: "A single conformally calibrated checkpoint was as efficient on MATR/CLO; on HUST the
sequential rule saved 12 points more than a checkpoint at cycle 100 but less than the best single checkpoint
(cycle 300)." Restate the contribution as *robustness to not knowing the right checkpoint*, and test that
claim directly (R10-03).

### R10-03 — MAJOR — Comparators are set up in ways that favour the proposed rule
*Location:* Section 3.4 (line 96), Section 4.4 (line 192 "knowledge that is not available before a campaign"),
Section 5 (line 227, 231, 233).
*Evidence and fixes:*
1. **The single checkpoint is fixed at cycle 100 on both datasets.** Cycle 100 is Severson's choice for MATR.
   On HUST, with L\* = 1,900, it is a poor default (40.6% saved, against 61.5% at cycle 300). The paper says the
   best checkpoint "is not available before a campaign". That is not true: it can be chosen **on the training
   cells**, which leaves the calibration guarantee intact. *My verification (UNVERIFIED in the sense of a
   pilot with few splits, but run with the paper's own code):*
   /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-p-c1-3/0430eaaa-3bde-56d0-96f2-4e2463d35486/scratchpad/selk.py. It uses the
   elastic net and fresh seeds. For each split and each checkpoint it computes a training-only score: the
   share of training cells with |log T − log L\*| > 1.28·s_m, weighted by the cycles saved. It picks the best
   checkpoint, then calibrates on calibration cells as usual.
   ```
   HUST (60 splits): proposed FPR .057 FRR .091 saved .543 | single@100 saved .357 | single@train-selected FPR .057 FRR .082 saved .494
   MATR/CLO (40 splits): proposed saved .807 | single@100 .798 | single@train-selected .758
   ```
   On HUST a crude, valid, data-driven single checkpoint closes about 74% of the gap. The remaining advantage is about 5
   points, not 12. A referee will ask for this baseline. Fix: add "single checkpoint selected on training
   cells (cross-validated)" as a comparator in E1, and report the gain over it.
2. **Bonferroni is set up so that it cannot decide.** With M = 16/17 and about 20 calibration cells per
   class it is +∞ by construction (line 92). That shows nothing beyond the arithmetic already in Remark (ii).
   Fair valid sequential baselines are: Bonferroni, Holm or α-spending on the sparse 2–4-look grids of E3.
   For example, M = 2 at α/2 = 0.05 needs only n0 ≥ 19, which is feasible on MATR/CLO. Fix: report them on the
   E3 grids.
3. **The parametric rule is the naive rule with Gaussian quantiles.** It applies z₁₋α at every look with no
   multiplicity correction. Its failure on HUST is therefore partly the same optional-stopping effect. It does
   not show that "model-based uncertainty is not a substitute" (line 233). Fix: add a Gaussian/Bayesian rule
   with a multiplicity-aware threshold, such as the max of a fitted Gaussian score process or Bonferroni on z.
   Alternatively, soften line 233.
4. **The interval rule is built to need EOL calibration** (`need_full = name == "interval_traj"`, runner.py:175).
   Censored or conformalised-survival lower bounds (refs 34–36, which the paper itself cites) use lifetimes
   truncated at a cut-off. So the "2.61× sooner calibration" compares against a baseline nobody would choose
   once the truncation idea is known. Fix: add a censored-conformal lower/upper-bound rule calibrated on
   min(T, L\*), or say explicitly that the truncation advantage is shared with conformalised survival
   methods.

### R10-04 — MAJOR — The cost metric changes between claims, and the abstract picks the most favourable number
*Location:* Abstract ("calibration campaigns ended 2.61 times sooner"); Section 4.3 (line 184); Section 5 (line 229);
Limitations "Cost model".
*Evidence:*
* Screening savings are given in **cell-cycles** (78.3%/50.7%). Calibration savings are given in **wall-clock**
  time (2.61×). On the *same* wall-clock basis (the time until the last test cell is decided), I recomputed from
  results/raw/E1_*_cells.csv.gz:
  ```
  matr_clo proposed:    mean 656.7 cycles, median 750; a cell goes to full observation in 84.8% of splits
  matr_clo single_k100: mean 398.8 cycles, median 100;  46.4% of splits
  hust     proposed:    mean 1887.5, median 1900; 99.6% of splits
  ```
  For a batch campaign, the sequential rule ends at L\* in most splits, so the wall-clock saving is about zero.
  On MATR/CLO the single-checkpoint rule finishes the campaign **1.65× sooner**. The cell-cycle saving is real
  as channel occupancy, but the reader is not told which resource is saved.
* The abstract gives 2.61 (MATR/CLO wall-clock). It omits 1.38 (HUST wall-clock) and the cell-cycle factors
  1.22 and 1.09 (line 184).
* The **training** set for the elastic net still needs cells cycled to EOL (T5: train wall-clock 2,130 and 2,656
  cycles, compared with 750 and 1,900 for calibration). So the whole preparatory campaign is not shortened at all
  unless a pre-trained model exists. Under shift such a model transfers poorly (23.9% saved cross-lab, R10-05).
  The truncated-training alternative (logistic) costs 10.8 and 10.7 points of saving (67.5 vs 78.3; 40.0 vs 50.7).

*Fix:* (i) Use one metric throughout, or report both channel-occupancy and wall-clock time for screening *and*
calibration. (ii) In the abstract, give the calibration factor as "1.4–2.6× in wall-clock (1.1–1.2× in
cell-cycles), provided a base predictor is already available". (iii) Add a channel-scheduling simulation
(finite number of channels, cells arriving in a queue) if throughput is the claimed benefit.

### R10-06 — MAJOR — The rule's specific weakness, fragility to noise present only at screening time, is absent from abstract and conclusions
*Location:* Section 4.6 (line 208) only. The abstract and conclusions say nothing.
*Evidence (T7, noise_test):* At 0.25 SD the proposed rule gave FPR/FRR 0.275/0.187 on HUST, against 0.139/0.139 for
the single-checkpoint rule. On MATR/CLO it gave 0.126/0.163 against 0.107/0.084. Already at 0.10 SD the proposed
rule breaks on HUST (FPR 0.128) and the single-checkpoint rule does not (0.096). Many looks give many chances to
cross the threshold. This weakness comes from exactly the property the paper sells. Different cyclers,
temperature chambers or a firmware change between the calibration and screening campaigns are routine.
*Fix:* Put it in the abstract and conclusions. Quantify a mitigation, for example calibrating with noise
inflation, re-measuring calibration cells on the screening equipment, or using sparse grids. Add a
non-Gaussian, physically motivated perturbation, such as temperature offset or a different current-sensor
resolution, rather than isotropic feature noise only.

### R10-07 — MAJOR — The headline numbers come from the most favourable operating point (α = 0.10, L\* at the median)
*Location:* Section 3.5 (line 100 "close to the median lifetimes, so that both classes are well represented");
Abstract; Section 4.7.
*Evidence (T10):* HUST α = β = 0.05 saved **0%** (no finite threshold). HUST L\* = 1,600/2,200 saved 15.5%/20.0%.
MATR/CLO α = 0.05 saved 53.8%. Qualification specifications are usually set so that most cells pass, which puts
L\* in a tail, and false-pass tolerances in qualification are usually below 10%. α = 0.01 would need at least 99
bad calibration cells.
*Fix:* In the abstract, add "at α = β = 0.10 and a specification near the median lifetime". Add a table of the
minimum calibration cells per class needed to obtain a given saving at α ∈ {0.01, 0.05, 0.10}, to show where the
method is practical.

### R10-08 — MAJOR — The unit and action of the decision are unclear (screening a single test cell)
*Location:* Introduction (line 21 "does this cell (or this design) meet a cycle-life specification"), Section 3.1.
*Evidence:* Cycle-life testing uses up the cell, so "passing" a test cell has no direct action. Decisions are
made about a design, protocol, lot or supplier, with replicate cells. The guarantee is per cell, class-conditional,
over an exchangeable population. A practitioner needs one of three things: (a) a per-design decision, (b) a
lot-acceptance statement, or (c) P(bad | passed), i.e. PPV/FDR, which depends on prevalence. None is evaluated.
Conformal selection with FDR control [37] is cited but not compared.
*Fix:* State the concrete use case, for example screening protocol candidates as in Attia et al. or incoming-lot
sampling. Report PPV/NPV at the observed prevalences and at realistic ones. Discuss or add a protocol-level
analysis on MATR, where some protocols have replicate cells.

### R10-10 — MAJOR (for a statistics-literate referee) — The theoretical novelty is thin and should be positioned more modestly
*Location:* Abstract "We prove"; Contribution 2; Section 2 "Gap"; Proposition 1.
*Evidence:* The proof is three lines. It is the split-conformal rank argument applied to the max over looks, plus
a union-event inclusion. The max-over-looks idea is the standard simultaneous-calibration device (for example in
conformal prediction for multiple time points, and anytime-valid or early-exit conformal [38]). The "truncated
calibration" remark follows at once from the thresholds depending only on Z. Line 40 already says "adapts". The
abstract and contribution list still read as a new theorem.
*Fix:* Replace "We prove" with "We show (a short adaptation of split-conformal arguments)". Cite
simultaneous/max-score conformal and Learn-then-Test style risk control explicitly. Present the contribution as an
engineering formulation plus a large evaluation, which is where the value is.

## Additional experiments a referee would demand (priority order)
1. A **single checkpoint selected on training cells** (cross-validated) as a valid baseline in E1, E4, E5 and E7.
   The pilot above suggests the HUST advantage shrinks from 12 to about 5 points.
2. **Valid sequential baselines on sparse grids**: Bonferroni, Holm or α-spending over 2–4 looks.
3. **Wall-clock and channel-throughput** simulation of a screening campaign (finite channels, queue of arriving
   cells), and the whole preparatory campaign including training-set cycling.
4. **Specification in the tail and smaller α**: the calibration sizes needed, and PPV/NPV at realistic prevalence.
5. A **censored-conformal (truncated) interval** baseline, so the calibration-time comparison is fair.
6. **Physically motivated test-only perturbations** (different cycler or temperature) and a mitigation for the
   sequential rule's fragility.
7. **Protocol- or design-level screening** on MATR (replicate cells per protocol), or a clear statement that only
   per-cell decisions are addressed.
8. Ideally a **second cell chemistry or format**. At minimum, retitle and qualify claims to LFP/graphite A123 cells.

## Overall recommendation (as a journal referee): **Major revision**
The method is correct and the evaluation is unusually thorough. But the framing (title, abstract, conclusions)
promises more than the data support, and the most relevant competitor (a well-chosen single conformal
checkpoint) is not given a fair, data-driven chance.

### The three changes that matter most
1. **Reframe the guarantee** (R10-01): drop "Guaranteed" from the title. State in the abstract that the guarantee
   is marginal and that per-campaign exceedance occurs in about 21–31% of splits per error type (and 43–51%
   for either one).
2. **Give the single-checkpoint rule a fair, valid, data-driven chance** (R10-02/03): select the checkpoint on
   training cells, add sparse-grid Bonferroni and α-spending, and report the gain of the sequential rule over these,
   including under shift and test-only noise (R10-05/06).
3. **Use one honest cost metric** (R10-04/07): wall-clock and channel occupancy for both screening and the whole
   preparatory campaign (including full-life training cells), and give the operating point (α, L\*) next to every
   headline saving.
