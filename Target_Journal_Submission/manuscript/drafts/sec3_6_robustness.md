### 3.6 Robustness and sensitivity

{{FIG:Fig8_robustness|Robustness of the screening rules (E4, {{CFG.n_secondary}} splits per configuration, elastic net). Larger of the mean false-pass and false-reject rates as a function of the calibration-set fraction, the training-set fraction and Gaussian feature noise (in multiples of each feature's training standard deviation) added either to calibration and test cells alike or to test cells only. Dashed line: nominal level 0.10. Cycling saved is given in {{STABREF:tables/T7_robustness.csv}}.}}

**Calibration and training set size.** The guarantee held for every calibration and training fraction tested ({{FIGREF:Fig8_robustness}}; {{STABREF:tables/T7_robustness.csv}}); what changed was efficiency. With only 10% of the MATR/CLO cells (about {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.1.proposed.ncal_bad}} bad and {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.1.proposed.ncal_good}} good cells) used for calibration, the rule saved {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.1.proposed.sav}}% of the cycling instead of {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.25.proposed.sav}}% with 25%, because with fewer than nine cells of a class the required order statistic does not exist and the rule cannot pass (or reject) at all in some splits. On HUST, a calibration fraction of 20% (about {{E4.h.cal_fraction.0.2.proposed.ncal_bad}} bad cells) reduced the saving to {{E4.h.cal_fraction.0.2.proposed.sav}}%. Smaller training sets lowered efficiency gradually (MATR/CLO {{E4.mc.train_fraction.0.2.proposed.sav}}% with 20% training cells versus {{E4.mc.train_fraction.0.5.proposed.sav}}% with 50%) without affecting validity. In practice, about 10–15 cells of each class are needed in the calibration set before the rule becomes efficient.

**Measurement noise.** When Gaussian noise was added to the features of calibration and test cells alike, the two remained exchangeable and the trajectory rule stayed valid at every noise level (false-pass rate {{E4.mc.noise_both.1.proposed.fpr}} and false-reject rate {{E4.mc.noise_both.1.proposed.frr}} on MATR/CLO with noise of one standard deviation), while its saving fell from {{E4.mc.noise_both.0.1.proposed.sav}}% to {{E4.mc.noise_both.1.proposed.sav}}%: noisier scores lead to wider thresholds and later decisions. The parametric rule, whose thresholds do not adapt to the data, became invalid ({{E4.mc.noise_both.1.gauss.fpr}} and {{E4.mc.noise_both.1.gauss.frr}}). Noise added to the test cells only — a model of screening cells on a noisier tester than the calibration cells — broke exchangeability, and every rule exceeded the targets: the trajectory rule reached {{E4.mc.noise_test.0.25.proposed.fpr}} and {{E4.mc.noise_test.0.25.proposed.frr}} at a quarter of a standard deviation and {{E4.mc.noise_test.1.proposed.fpr}} and {{E4.mc.noise_test.1.proposed.frr}} at one standard deviation. Calibration cells must therefore be measured on the same equipment and protocol as the cells being screened; Section 3.7 shows the same requirement for batch and laboratory shift.
