### 4.1 Data and base predictors

{{FIG:Fig2_lifetime_distributions|Cycle-life distributions of the two data sets and the specifications used in the main experiments. The specifications split both data sets roughly in half ({{FIGREF:Fig2_lifetime_distributions}}). The accuracy of the elastic-net predictor improved steadily with the number of observed cycles ({{FIGREF:Fig3_base_model_accuracy}}): on the test cells of all MATR/CLO splits, the mean absolute percentage error of the predicted cycle life fell from {{E1ACC.mc.k20}}% at 20 cycles to {{E1ACC.mc.k100}}% at 100 cycles and {{E1ACC.mc.k300}}% at 300 cycles; on HUST it was {{E1ACC.h.k20}}%, {{E1ACC.h.k100}}% and {{E1ACC.h.k1000}}% at 20, 100 and 1000 cycles. The error at 100 cycles on MATR/CLO is close to the 9.1% reported by Severson et al. for their full model on a fixed split [@severson2019], although our pooled data, random splits and feature set (no resistance features) differ from theirs. The score scale $s_k$ tracks the decrease of prediction error, which is what normalisation (Equation 2) exploits.

### 4.2 Validity of the screening decisions

{{FIG:Fig4_validity_and_efficiency|Validity (top) and efficiency (bottom) of the screening rules in experiment E1 ({{CFG.n_main}} random splits per data set, elastic net, $\alpha=\beta=0.10$). {{TABLE:tables/T2_main_results.csv|Screening results of experiment E1 (elastic net, {{CFG.n_main}} random splits per data set, $\alpha=\beta=0.10$). FPR: false-pass rate among bad test cells; FRR: false-reject rate among good test cells; "upper" = upper limit of the 95% confidence interval of the mean over splits; early: share of test cells decided by the model before $\min(T,L^\ast)$; saved: fraction of cycling time saved relative to cycling every test cell to $\min(T,L^\ast)$.|dataset:Data set:s,method:Rule:s,fpr_mean:FPR:.3f,fpr_ci_hi:FPR upper:.3f,frr_mean:FRR:.3f,frr_ci_hi:FRR upper:.3f,accuracy_mean:Accuracy:.3f,early_rate_mean:Early:pct1,time_saving_mean:Saved:pct1,mean_time_mean:Cycles to decision:.0f}}

The trajectory-calibrated rule met both error targets on both data sets ({{FIGREF:Fig4_validity_and_efficiency}}, {{TABREF:tables/T2_main_results.csv}}). On MATR/CLO its mean false-pass rate was {{E1.mc.proposed.fpr}} (95% CI {{E1.mc.proposed.fpr_lo}}–{{E1.mc.proposed.fpr_hi}}) and its false-reject rate {{E1.mc.proposed.frr}} ({{E1.mc.proposed.frr_lo}}–{{E1.mc.proposed.frr_hi}}); on HUST they were {{E1.h.proposed.fpr}} ({{E1.h.proposed.fpr_lo}}–{{E1.h.proposed.fpr_hi}}) and {{E1.h.proposed.frr}} ({{E1.h.proposed.frr_lo}}–{{E1.h.proposed.frr_hi}}). The rates are below the nominal 0.10 because the maximum statistic is conservative when the scores of later checkpoints add little beyond earlier ones. As the theory predicts, the bound holds on average rather than in every split: the realised false-pass rate exceeded 0.10 in {{E1.mc.proposed.fpr_above}}% (MATR/CLO) and {{E1.h.proposed.fpr_above}}% (HUST) of the splits, with about {{E1.mc.proposed.ncal_bad}} and {{E1.h.proposed.ncal_bad}} bad cells, respectively, in the test sets' companion calibration sets. The single-checkpoint conformal rule, the proposed rule without normalisation and the lifetime-interval rule were also valid, as Proposition 1 (with $M=1$) and conformal theory imply.

The rules without a valid calibration behaved very differently. Using individually valid per-checkpoint thresholds sequentially (naive sequential) inflated the false-pass rate to {{E1.mc.naive.fpr}} on MATR/CLO and {{E1.h.naive.fpr}} on HUST, and the false-reject rate to {{E1.mc.naive.frr}} and {{E1.h.naive.frr}} — up to {{RATIO.naive_max}} times the nominal level. The Severson-style point rule at cycle 100 happened to stay below 0.10 on MATR/CLO ({{E1.mc.point_k100.fpr}} and {{E1.mc.point_k100.frr}}) but not on HUST ({{E1.h.point_k100.fpr}} and {{E1.h.point_k100.frr}}). The parametric rule with Gaussian thresholds on the same normalised scores was conservative on MATR/CLO ({{E1.mc.gauss.fpr}}, {{E1.mc.gauss.frr}}) and violated the false-reject target on HUST ({{E1.h.gauss.frr}}, upper confidence limit {{E1.h.gauss.frr_hi}}). Bonferroni calibration never passed or rejected a cell, because with about {{E1.mc.bonf.ncal_bad}} bad and {{E1.mc.bonf.ncal_good}} good calibration cells the required order statistic at level $\alpha/M$ does not exist.

On MATR/CLO the proposed rule decided {{E1.mc.proposed.early}}% of the test cells from their early cycles and saved {{E1.mc.proposed.sav}}% of the cycling needed to observe every cell to $\min(T,L^\ast)$ (mean {{E1.mc.proposed.time}} cycles per cell instead of {{D.mc.mean_full_cost}}); half of the cells were decided within {{E1.mc.proposed.time_median}} cycles and {{E1.mc.proposed.dec_by_100}}% by cycle 100 ({{FIGREF:Fig5_decision_time}}). On HUST, whose cells live longer and where the specification is 1,900 cycles, it decided {{E1.h.proposed.early}}% of the cells early and saved {{E1.h.proposed.sav}}% (mean {{E1.h.proposed.time}} instead of {{D.h.mean_full_cost}} cycles).

Among the valid rules, the proposed rule saved more cycling than the lifetime-interval rule on both data sets (by {{P.mc.interval_traj.diff}} and {{P.h.interval_traj.diff}} percentage points; Holm-adjusted $p$ = {{P.mc.interval_traj.padj}} and {{P.h.interval_traj.padj}}; {{TABREF:tables/T3_paired_efficiency.csv}}), and more than the unnormalised variant (by {{P.mc.traj_unnorm.diff}} and {{P.h.traj_unnorm.diff}} points). Against the single-checkpoint rule the result depended on the data set: on HUST the proposed rule saved {{P.h.single_k100.diff}} points more (95% CI {{P.h.single_k100.lo}} to {{P.h.single_k100.hi}}; $p$ = {{P.h.single_k100.padj}}; $r$ = {{P.h.single_k100.r}}), whereas on MATR/CLO the difference was {{P.mc.single_k100.diff}} points (95% CI {{P.mc.single_k100.lo}} to {{P.mc.single_k100.hi}}; $p$ = {{P.mc.single_k100.padj}}), i.e. not significant. The rules that saved more time than the proposed one — naive sequential, point prediction and, on HUST, the parametric rule — did so by violating at least one error target on at least one data set.

Total calibration cell-cycles fell by factors of {{C.mc.cal_cycles_ratio}} and {{C.h.cal_cycles_ratio}}.

### 4.4 Base predictors and checkpoint grid

**Other base predictors (E2).** The guarantee does not depend on the base predictor, and the data agree ({{STABREF:tables/T4_base_models.csv}}). With logistic regression trained on truncated cells, gradient boosting, random forests, Gaussian-process regression and a one-dimensional CNN, the proposed rule's mean false-pass rate ranged from {{E2.mc.rf.proposed.fpr}} to {{E2.mc.gp.proposed.fpr}} on MATR/CLO and from {{E2.h.rf.proposed.fpr}} to {{E2.h.gbr.proposed.fpr}} on HUST, and its false-reject rate from {{E2.mc.logit.proposed.frr}} to {{E2.mc.rf.proposed.frr}} and from {{E2.h.gbr.proposed.frr}} to {{E2.h.cnn.proposed.frr}}. The one mean above 0.10 — the CNN on HUST, {{E2.h.cnn.proposed.frr}} (95% CI {{E2.h.cnn.proposed.frr_lo}}–{{E2.h.cnn.proposed.frr_hi}}) — comes from only {{E2.h.cnn.proposed.nsplits}} splits and is consistent with the nominal level. The naive sequential rule exceeded both targets with every predictor (false-pass rate {{E2.mc.logit.naive.fpr}}–{{E2.mc.rf.naive.fpr}} on MATR/CLO and {{E2.h.rf.naive.fpr}}–{{E2.h.gp.naive.fpr}} on HUST), and the parametric rule was unsafe with several predictors (HUST Gaussian process: {{E2.h.gp.gauss.fpr}} and {{E2.h.gp.gauss.frr}}; HUST CNN: {{E2.h.cnn.gauss.fpr}} and {{E2.h.cnn.gauss.frr}}). The predictor mattered for efficiency: on MATR/CLO the saved cycling ranged from {{E2.mc.logit.proposed.sav}}% (logistic regression on truncated training cells) to {{E2.mc.rf.proposed.sav}}% (random forest), against {{E1.mc.proposed.sav}}% for the elastic net; on HUST from {{E2.h.logit.proposed.sav}}% to {{E2.h.cnn.proposed.sav}}% against {{E1.h.proposed.sav}}%. Truncating the training cells as well as the calibration cells therefore keeps the guarantee but costs about ten percentage points of savings.

{{FIG:Fig7_ablation_checkpoint_grid|Checkpoint-grid ablation (E3, {{CFG.n_secondary}} splits per data set, elastic net): cycling time saved by the proposed rule, its unnormalised variant and the naive sequential rule for each grid. Grids with a single checkpoint make the three rules identical. The naive rule's savings are not comparable: it violates the error targets on HUST for every grid with more than one checkpoint and on MATR/CLO for the dense grids.}}

**Checkpoint grid (E3).** With a single checkpoint the three rules coincide and are valid; every additional look inflated the naive rule's error and left the proposed rule valid ({{FIGREF:Fig7_ablation_checkpoint_grid}}, {{STABREF:tables/T6_ablation_grid.csv}}). On HUST the naive false-pass rate rose from {{E3.h.single_100.naive.fpr}} (one checkpoint) to {{E3.h.pair_100_1000.naive.fpr}} (two), {{E3.h.sparse_100_300_600_1000.naive.fpr}} (four) and {{E3.h.all17.naive.fpr}} (all 17), while the proposed rule stayed between {{E3.h.early_20to100.proposed.fpr}} and {{E3.h.single_100.proposed.fpr}}. The grid affected efficiency more than validity. A sparse grid saved the most of all grids tested on MATR/CLO ({{E3.mc.sparse_50_100_200_300.proposed.sav}}% with checkpoints 50, 100, 200 and 300, versus {{E3.mc.all16.proposed.sav}}% with all 16), and a well-chosen single checkpoint can beat the full grid: on HUST a single look at cycle 300 saved {{E3.h.single_300.proposed.sav}}%, compared with {{E3.h.all17.proposed.sav}}% for all 17 checkpoints, but a single look at cycle 100 or 1000 saved only {{E3.h.single_100.proposed.sav}}% or {{E3.h.single_1000.proposed.sav}}%. The best of the single checkpoints tested differed between the data sets (cycle 50 on MATR/CLO, cycle 300 on HUST) and could not have been chosen without the data used for evaluation. Score normalisation mattered most with dense grids (MATR/CLO, all 16 checkpoints: {{E3.mc.all16.proposed.sav}}% with and {{E3.mc.all16.traj_unnorm.sav}}% without normalisation), where unnormalised early scores, whose spread is large, dominate the maximum.

### 4.5 Calibration-set size, error levels and training-conditional control

{{FIG:Fig8_robustness|Robustness (E4, {{CFG.n_secondary}} splits per setting): the larger of the mean false-pass and false-reject rates as a function of the calibration fraction, the training fraction, and Gaussian feature noise (in units of each feature's standard deviation) added to calibration and test cells or to test cells only. Dashed line: nominal level 0.10.}}

**Calibration and training size (E4).** The proposed rule stayed valid for every calibration and training fraction ({{FIGREF:Fig8_robustness}}, {{STABREF:tables/T7_robustness.csv}}). Small calibration sets did not break the guarantee but made the rule abstain: with about {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.1.proposed.ncal_bad}} bad calibration cells (MATR/CLO, calibration fraction 0.10) the order statistic needed to pass a cell often did not exist, so no cell was passed in {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.1.proposed.never_pass}}% of the splits and the saving fell to {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.1.proposed.sav}}% (from {{E4.mc.cal_fraction.0.25.proposed.sav}}% at 0.25); on HUST with about {{E4.h.cal_fraction.0.2.proposed.ncal_bad}} bad calibration cells the saving was {{E4.h.cal_fraction.0.2.proposed.sav}}%. Smaller training sets reduced the saving moderately (MATR/CLO: {{E4.mc.train_fraction.0.2.proposed.sav}}% with 20% of the cells for training, {{E4.mc.train_fraction.0.5.proposed.sav}}% with 50%). The parametric rule violated the false-reject target on HUST for every calibration fraction (≥ {{E4.h.cal_fraction.0.4.gauss.frr}}).

{{FIG:Fig9_sensitivity_Lstar|Sensitivity to the specification (E7, proposed rule, $\alpha=\beta=0.10$, {{CFG.n_secondary}} splits): mean false-pass and false-reject rates and cycling time saved for three values of $L^\ast$ per data set.}}

**Specification and error levels (E7).** Moving $L^\ast$ changes the class balance ({{FIGREF:Fig9_sensitivity_Lstar}}, {{STABREF:tables/T10_sensitivity.csv}}). On MATR/CLO the rule stayed valid for $L^\ast$ = 550, 750 and 900 cycles (savings {{E7.mc.L550.proposed.sav}}%, {{E7.mc.L750.proposed.sav}}% and {{E7.mc.L900.proposed.sav}}%). On HUST, with $L^\ast$ = 1,600 only about {{E7.h.L1600.proposed.ncal_bad}} calibration cells were bad, too few to pass any cell at $\alpha$ = 0.10 (no pass in {{E7.h.L1600.proposed.never_pass}}% of the splits), and with $L^\ast$ = 2,200 only about {{E7.h.L2200.proposed.ncal_good}} were good; the rule remained valid but saved only {{E7.h.L1600.proposed.sav}}% and {{E7.h.L2200.proposed.sav}}%. The parametric rule, by contrast, violated one of the targets at both alternative specifications on HUST (false-pass rate {{E7.h.L1600.gauss.fpr}} at 1,600; false-reject rate {{E7.h.L2200.gauss.frr}} at 2,200). Stricter error levels need more calibration cells: at $\alpha=\beta=0.05$ the rule decided nothing early on HUST (about {{E7.h.ab_0.05_0.05.proposed.ncal_bad}} cells per class; at least 19 are needed) and saved {{E7.mc.ab_0.05_0.05.proposed.sav}}% on MATR/CLO with error rates {{E7.mc.ab_0.05_0.05.proposed.fpr}} and {{E7.mc.ab_0.05_0.05.proposed.frr}}. At $\alpha=\beta=0.20$ the rule was valid on both data sets ({{E7.mc.ab_0.2_0.2.proposed.fpr}}/{{E7.mc.ab_0.2_0.2.proposed.frr}} and {{E7.h.ab_0.2_0.2.proposed.fpr}}/{{E7.h.ab_0.2_0.2.proposed.frr}}) and saved {{E7.mc.ab_0.2_0.2.proposed.sav}}% and {{E7.h.ab_0.2_0.2.proposed.sav}}%.

**Training-conditional control (E10).** The marginal guarantee allows the error of a particular calibration set to exceed the target: the realised false-pass rate of the proposed rule was above 0.10 in {{E10.mc.default.proposed.fpr_above}}% of the MATR/CLO splits and {{E10.h.default.proposed.fpr_above}}% of the HUST splits ({{STABREF:tables/T12_pac.csv}}), somewhat below the analytic probabilities for continuous scores ({{STABREF:tables/T13_conditional_error_analytic.csv}}; e.g. {{PAC.p_exceed_n20}}% for 20 cells), because the trajectory maximum is conservative. The PAC variant, which chooses the rank so that the conditional error exceeds $\alpha$ with probability at most 0.1, reduced these shares to {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.fpr_above}}% and {{E10.h.default.traj_pac.fpr_above}}%, but at a high price: with the default calibration sets (about {{E10.mc.default.proposed.ncal_bad}} bad cells on MATR/CLO and {{E10.h.default.proposed.ncal_bad}} on HUST, fewer than the 22 required) it saved {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.sav}}% on MATR/CLO and nothing on HUST. With enlarged calibration sets (about {{E10.mc.large_cal.proposed.ncal_bad}} and {{E10.h.large_cal.proposed.ncal_bad}} bad cells) it saved {{E10.mc.large_cal.traj_pac.sav}}% and {{E10.h.large_cal.traj_pac.sav}}%, against {{E10.mc.large_cal.proposed.sav}}% and {{E10.h.large_cal.proposed.sav}}% for the marginal rule.

### 4.6 Distribution shift

{{FIG:Fig10_distribution_shift|Batch and laboratory shift (E5, {{CFG.n_secondary}} splits per scenario): mean false-pass (left) and false-reject (right) rates with 95% confidence intervals. Batch shift: models trained and calibrated on MATR batches 1–2 and applied to batch 3 or the CLO batch (b4), either with source calibration or after recalibration with $n$ = 20 or 30 target cells cycled to $L^\ast$. Laboratory shift: capacity-trajectory features; HUST test cells screened with a model trained on MATR/CLO or on HUST, both calibrated with HUST cells. Dashed line: nominal level 0.10.}}

**Batch shift (E5).** When a model trained and calibrated on the first two MATR batches was used to screen a later batch, the guarantee failed, as expected when calibration and test cells are not exchangeable ({{FIGREF:Fig10_distribution_shift}}, {{STABREF:tables/T8_shift.csv}}). The proposed rule falsely rejected {{E5.b3_source.proposed.frr}} of the good cells of batch 3 and {{E5.b4_source.proposed.frr}} of those of the CLO batch, and the single-checkpoint rule falsely passed {{E5.b3_source.single_k100.fpr}} and {{E5.b4_source.single_k100.fpr}} of the bad cells; every rule violated at least one target on at least one batch. Recalibration with target cells cycled only to $L^\ast$, keeping the source model, restored validity: with 30 CLO cells the error rates were {{E5.b4_target30.proposed.fpr}} and {{E5.b4_target30.proposed.frr}}, with a saving of {{E5.b4_target30.proposed.sav}}%. With 20 target cells, or for batch 3, which contains few bad cells at this specification (about {{E5.b3_target20.proposed.ncal_bad}} among 20), the rule remained valid by passing no cell early and saved little ({{E5.b3_target20.proposed.sav}}–{{E5.b3_target30.proposed.sav}}%). The parametric rule, recalibrated in the same way, kept a false-reject rate above 0.79.

**Laboratory shift (E5) and unseen protocols (E9).** A model trained on MATR/CLO capacity trajectories and calibrated with HUST cells screened HUST cells validly ({{E5.xlab_source.proposed.fpr}} and {{E5.xlab_source.proposed.frr}}), saving {{E5.xlab_source.proposed.sav}}% compared with {{E5.xlab_target.proposed.sav}}% when the model was trained on HUST cells as well; the point-prediction rule was invalid in both cases ({{E5.xlab_source.point_k100.fpr}} and {{E5.xlab_source.point_k100.frr}} with the MATR model). When whole charging protocols were held out — {{E9.n_groups}} protocol groups of MATR/CLO assigned to training, calibration or test — the proposed rule kept its targets ({{E9.mc.proposed.fpr}}, 95% CI {{E9.mc.proposed.fpr_lo}}–{{E9.mc.proposed.fpr_hi}}; {{E9.mc.proposed.frr}}, {{E9.mc.proposed.frr_lo}}–{{E9.mc.proposed.frr_hi}}) and saved {{E9.mc.proposed.sav}}%, while the naive rule again exceeded both ({{E9.mc.naive.fpr}} and {{E9.mc.naive.frr}}; {{STABREF:tables/T11_protocol_grouped.csv}}). Because protocol groups were assigned to calibration and test at random, exchangeability holds at the level of protocols, and the guarantee held although no test protocol appeared among the training or calibration cells. Calibration cells therefore have to come from the population being screened, but that population can span protocols not seen in training.

### 4.7 Measurement noise and computational cost

**Noise (E4).** Gaussian noise added to the features of calibration and test cells alike left the proposed rule valid at every level up to one feature standard deviation and only reduced the saving (MATR/CLO: {{E4.mc.noise_both.0.1.proposed.sav}}% at 0.1 SD to {{E4.mc.noise_both.1.proposed.sav}}% at 1 SD; HUST: {{E4.h.noise_both.0.1.proposed.sav}}% to {{E4.h.noise_both.1.proposed.sav}}%), whereas the parametric rule became invalid already at 0.25 SD ({{FIGREF:Fig8_robustness}}). Noise added to the test cells only — a calibration set measured on a cleaner instrument than the cells screened — violates exchangeability, and it hurt the proposed rule more than the single-checkpoint rule, because the maximum over many noisy checkpoints is more likely to cross a threshold: at 0.25 SD the false-pass rates were {{E4.mc.noise_test.0.25.proposed.fpr}} and {{E4.mc.noise_test.0.25.single_k100.fpr}} on MATR/CLO and {{E4.h.noise_test.0.25.proposed.fpr}} and {{E4.h.noise_test.0.25.single_k100.fpr}} on HUST. Calibration and screening must use the same measurement chain.

**Cost (E6).** Fitting the elastic-net predictors for all checkpoints of one split took {{E6.mc.en.sec}} s (MATR/CLO) and {{E6.h.en.sec}} s (HUST) on four CPU cores, and the slowest predictor, the random forest, {{E6.mc.rf.sec}} s and {{E6.h.rf.sec}} s ({{STABREF:tables/T9_compute.csv}}, {{SFIGREF:Fig14_compute}}). The calibration step itself took {{E6.mc.calibration_only_n4000.cal_ms}} ms even with 4,000 calibration cells, and screening 1,000 cells about {{E6.mc.calibration_only_n400.decide_ms}} ms. The procedure adds no measurable cost to an existing prediction pipeline.

### 4.8 Error analysis

{{FIG:Fig11_error_analysis|Error frequency of the proposed rule for each cell, across the splits in which the cell was a test cell (E1), against the distance of its cycle life from the specification, $\log_{10}(T/L^\ast)$. Bad cells (left) can only be falsely passed; good cells (right) can only be falsely rejected.}}

Errors concentrated near the specification ({{FIGREF:Fig11_error_analysis}}): the Spearman correlation between $|\log_{10}(T/L^\ast)|$ and a cell's error frequency was {{E8.mc.rho}} on MATR/CLO ($p$ = {{E8.mc.p}}) and {{E8.h.rho}} on HUST ($p$ = {{E8.h.p}}). Cells within 0.05 decades of $L^\ast$ (about ±12% in cycle life) had a mean error frequency of {{E8.mc.err_near}} on MATR/CLO compared with {{E8.mc.err_far}} for cells more than 0.1 decades away, and they accounted for {{E8.mc.share_err_near}}% of all errors. Errors far from $L^\ast$ came from a few cells whose early-cycle behaviour was not representative of their lifetime ({{STABREF:tables/E8_worst_cells_matr_clo.csv}}): the shortest-lived MATR cell (148 cycles) failed abruptly after early cycles that resembled those of ordinary cells and was falsely passed in most splits in which it was tested, and one long-lived cell (1,315 cycles) was falsely rejected in most splits because its early capacity-difference signature resembled that of short-lived cells. No early-cycle screening rule can classify such cells correctly from these features; the guarantee bounds how often such cells are mis-screened in aggregate but cannot identify them. On HUST the most frequent errors involved cells within about 6% of $L^\ast$ ({{STABREF:tables/E8_worst_cells_hust.csv}}).
