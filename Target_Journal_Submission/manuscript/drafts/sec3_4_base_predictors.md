The guarantee of Proposition 1 does not depend on the base predictor, and the experiments agree ({{TABREF:tables/T4_base_models.csv}}). With logistic regression, gradient-boosted trees, random forests, a Gaussian process and a convolutional network, the proposed rule kept both mean error rates at or below the nominal 0.10 in every case except the CNN on HUST, whose false-reject rate of {{E2.h.cnn.proposed.frr}} (upper 95% limit {{E2.h.cnn.proposed.frr_hi}}) rests on only {{CFG.n_cnn}} splits and is compatible with the target within Monte-Carlo error. The naive sequential rule exceeded the targets with every model. The choice of model mattered for efficiency, not for validity: on MATR/CLO the trajectory rule saved {{E2.mc.gbr.proposed.sav}}% (GBR), {{E2.mc.rf.proposed.sav}}% (RF), {{E2.mc.gp.proposed.sav}}% (GP) and {{E2.mc.cnn.proposed.sav}}% (CNN) of the cycling, more than the single-checkpoint rule with the same model ({{E2.mc.gbr.single_k100.sav}}%, {{E2.mc.rf.single_k100.sav}}%, {{E2.mc.gp.single_k100.sav}}% and {{E2.mc.cnn.single_k100.sav}}%), so the elastic net was the only MATR/CLO model for which a fixed checkpoint at cycle 100 was as efficient as the sequential rule. On HUST the trajectory rule saved more than the single-checkpoint rule with every model.

Model-based uncertainty again failed to provide control. The Bayesian rule of the Gaussian process, which passes a cell when its posterior probability of reaching $L^\ast$ exceeds $1-\alpha$, had false-pass and false-reject rates of {{E2.h.gp.gauss.fpr}} and {{E2.h.gp.gauss.frr}} on HUST and a false-reject rate of {{E2.mc.gp.gauss.frr}} on MATR/CLO: the posterior was over-confident for cells unlike the training cells. Logistic regression, trained only on the class of each cell and therefore on training cells cycled to $\min(T,L^\ast)$ rather than to end of life, gave valid decisions but saved less cycling ({{E2.mc.logit.proposed.sav}}% on MATR/CLO and {{E2.h.logit.proposed.sav}}% on HUST) than the elastic net trained on full lifetimes ({{E1.mc.proposed.sav}}% and {{E1.h.proposed.sav}}%). A laboratory that has no full-lifetime history can therefore start screening with a truncated training campaign, at the cost of less efficient decisions.
