### 3.8 Calibration-set-conditional control and computation

**How often a single campaign misses the target.** Proposition 1 bounds error rates on average over calibration sets. For one particular calibration set the conditional false-pass probability can exceed $\alpha$, and with tens of calibration cells it does so often: in the main experiment the realised false-pass rate on the test cells exceeded 0.10 in {{E1.mc.proposed.fpr_above}}% (MATR/CLO) and {{E1.h.proposed.fpr_above}}% (HUST) of the splits, the false-reject rate in {{E1.mc.proposed.frr_above}}% and {{E1.h.proposed.frr_above}}%, and at least one of the two in {{E1.mc.proposed.either_above}}% and {{E1.h.proposed.either_above}}%. Part of this is test-set sampling noise (about {{E1.mc.proposed.ntest_bad}} bad and {{E1.mc.proposed.ntest_good}} good test cells per split on MATR/CLO), and part is the variability of the calibrated threshold itself: for 20 calibration cells of a class, the probability that the conditional error probability of a marginally calibrated threshold exceeds $\alpha=0.10$ is {{PAC.p_exceed_n20}}% ({{STABREF:tables/T13_conditional_error_analytic.csv}}). The single-checkpoint and lifetime-interval rules share this property; it is inherent to marginal conformal guarantees with small calibration sets.

**Training-conditional (PAC) calibration.** The PAC variant of Section 2.4 ($\delta=0.10$) targets this directly, at a large cost in efficiency ({{STABREF:tables/T12_pac.csv}}). On MATR/CLO, with about {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.ncal_bad}} bad and {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.ncal_good}} good calibration cells, it lowered the mean error rates to {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.fpr}} and {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.frr}} and saved {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.sav}}% of the cycling instead of {{E10.mc.default.proposed.sav}}%; with about {{E10.mc.large_cal.traj_pac.ncal_bad}} bad and {{E10.mc.large_cal.traj_pac.ncal_good}} good calibration cells it saved {{E10.mc.large_cal.traj_pac.sav}}% (marginal rule {{E10.mc.large_cal.proposed.sav}}%). On HUST, with about {{E10.h.default.traj_pac.ncal_bad}} cells per class, no finite PAC threshold exists (at least 22 are needed at $\alpha=\delta=0.10$) and the rule never decided; with about {{E10.h.large_cal.traj_pac.ncal_bad}} per class it passed no cell in {{E10.h.large_cal.traj_pac.never_pass}}% of the splits and saved {{E10.h.large_cal.traj_pac.sav}}%. The share of splits in which the realised test-set error exceeded 0.10 fell to {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.fpr_above}}% (false pass) and {{E10.mc.default.traj_pac.frr_above}}% (false reject) on MATR/CLO. This split-level share is only a proxy for the PAC property, because the realised rate on 20–40 test cells also varies with the test sample; it is consistent with, but does not verify, the $\delta=0.10$ bound. Calibration-set-conditional control is therefore practical only with several tens of calibration cells per class; with fewer, users should accept the marginal guarantee and plan for the per-campaign variability reported above.
